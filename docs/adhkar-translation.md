# Morning and evening adhkar: transliteration, translation and sources

Status: draft prepared by Claude on 7 Nov 2026 for the user's review. **Nothing here ships until the user has checked it.**

The Arabic is copied unchanged from `docs/tools-content.md`, section 1. Words marked (*) are the evening wording.

## How this was prepared

**Transliteration rules (used for all 13):**
- Plain English letters, no diacritics. `'` stands for both 'ayn and hamza (popular Hisn al-Muslim style). `dh` = ذ, `th` = ث, `kh` = خ, `gh` = غ, `sh` = ش. Emphatic letters (ص ض ط ظ) are written s, d, t, z. Long vowels are not marked.
- The words run on as they are read aloud: case endings are written in full, and only the **last word of each dhikr** is in its pause form (e.g. "...illa ant").
- The "al-" of the sun letters is assimilated, as it is pronounced (e.g. "as-sama'i", "ash-shukr"). No other tajwid assimilation (iqlab, idgham) is shown, so "akhidhun bi-..." and "siratin mustaqim", not "akhidhum-bi" or "siratim-mustaqim".
- Hamzat al-wasl is dropped where it is silent (e.g. "ma'as-mihi", "mastata'tu").
- Attached prepositions take a hyphen (bi-, fa-), except bika, billahi and words with the article (bil-, fil-, minal-).

**Translations compared:** (1) the Darussalam English *Fortress of the Muslim* (Hisn al-Muslim); (2) the English on Sunnah.com for the source hadith (Ahmad Hasan for Abu Dawud, the Darussalam translation for Tirmidhi, Muhsin Khan for Bukhari, Abdul Hamid Siddiqui for Muslim). For several items I checked these against an open copy of the same translations: Abu Dawud 5068 and 5088, Tirmidhi 3391 and Bukhari 6306. For the meanings of disputed words I also used the classical commentaries (Ibn Hajar's *Fath al-Bari*, al-Nawawi's *al-Adhkar*, Mulla 'Ali al-Qari's *Mirqat*). The renderings below are my own and stay close to the Arabic. None is copied from any of these.

**How the references were checked:** Sunnah.com blocked automated access, so I checked the Arabic text in an open copy of the same collections (the fawazahmed0 hadith-api dataset, which uses Sunnah.com numbering). Secondary references (Dorar.net, hadithanswers.com, surahquran.com) were used where no primary text could be reached. Anything I could not confirm is marked **[unverified]**. As the user asked, no gradings are given: only the collection and number.

## 1. Bismillahil-ladhi la yadurru (3 times)

**Arabic:** بِسْمِ اللهِ الَّذِي لَا يَضُرُّ مَعَ اسْمِهِ شَيْءٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Bismillahil-ladhi la yadurru ma'as-mihi shay'un fil-ardi wa la fis-sama'i, wa huwas-Sami'ul-'Alim.

**Translation:** In the name of Allah, with whose name nothing on earth or in the heaven causes harm, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.

**Source:** Abu Dawud 5088 and Tirmidhi 3388, both from 'Uthman ibn 'Affan. Also Ibn Majah 3869 [unverified].

**Virtue:** Whoever says it three times in the morning and three in the evening, nothing will harm him (Tirmidhi 3388). In Abu Dawud 5088: said three times, he will not be struck by a sudden affliction until morning, and if said in the morning, until evening.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- *ma'a ismihi*: literally "along with His name", meaning "when His name is mentioned". Sunnah.com (Abu Dawud) renders it "when Whose name is mentioned" and Darussalam "with Whose Name". I kept the literal "with whose name".
- *as-sama'*: the Arabic is singular, so I wrote "the heaven". Some translators write "the heavens".
- The count of 3 matches both narrations. The user's Arabic matches the narration.

## 2. Raditu billahi Rabban (3 times)

**Arabic:** رَضِيتُ بِاللهِ رَبًّا وَبِالْإِسْلَامِ دِينًا وَبِمُحَمَّدٍ نَبِيًّا

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Raditu billahi Rabban, wa bil-Islami dinan, wa bi-Muhammadin nabiyya.

**Translation:** I am pleased with Allah as Lord, with Islam as religion, and with Muhammad as Prophet.

**Source:**
- Tirmidhi 3389, from Thawban. It is said in the evening, with "nabiyyan" and no count.
- Ibn Majah 3870, from Abu Sallam from a servant of the Prophet ﷺ. It is said morning and evening, with "raditu ... nabiyyan" and no count.
- Abu Dawud 5072 has the wording "رَضِينَا ... وَبِمُحَمَّدٍ رَسُولًا" and no count.
- The count of **three times** is in Musnad Ahmad (18967–18969 in one numbering; 23111 in Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut's edition) and in al-Nasa'i's *al-Sunan al-Kubra* 9747 and 10324. These are reported by hadithanswers.com and a Dorar summary. I did not check them in the primary text: **[unverified]**.

**Virtue:** It is a right upon Allah to please him (Tirmidhi 3389). In Ibn Majah 3870 and Abu Dawud 5072: on the Day of Resurrection.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- *raditu* is a past-tense verb that describes a present, settled state, so I wrote "I am pleased". Others write "I am content" or "I have accepted".
- **The final word:** the user's "nabiyyan" matches Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Ahmad and al-Nasa'i. Abu Dawud has "rasulan" (with "radina"). Al-Nawawi (*al-Adhkar*) recommends saying "nabiyyan wa rasulan" to join the narrations, as hadithanswers.com reports. This is shared for information only.
- **The count:** 3 is supported by the Ahmad and Nasa'i narrations (secondary report). The narrations in Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah and Abu Dawud have no count. Mulla 'Ali al-Qari (*Mirqat*) noted that "thalathan" might refer to the three phrases rather than three repetitions (hadithanswers.com).

## 3. Allahumma bika asbahna (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ بِكَ أَصْبَحْنَا (أَمْسَيْنَا*) وَبِكَ أَمْسَيْنَا (أَصْبَحْنَا*) وَبِكَ نَحْيَا وَبِكَ نَمُوتُ وَإِلَيْكَ النُّشُورُ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration, morning:** Allahumma bika asbahna, wa bika amsayna, wa bika nahya, wa bika namutu, wa ilaykan-nushur.

**Transliteration, evening:** Allahumma bika amsayna, wa bika asbahna, wa bika nahya, wa bika namutu, wa ilaykan-nushur.

**Translation, morning:** O Allah, by You we enter the morning and by You we enter the evening, by You we live and by You we die, and to You is the resurrection.

**Translation, evening:** O Allah, by You we enter the evening and by You we enter the morning, by You we live and by You we die, and to You is the resurrection.

**Source:** Abu Hurayrah narrates it in all of these.
- Abu Dawud 5068.
- Tirmidhi 3391.
- Ibn Majah 3868.
- Al-Bukhari, *al-Adab al-Mufrad* 1199, per Dorar and tohed.com: **[unverified in the primary text]**.

**Virtue:** none stated in the hadith.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **The ending, stated neutrally:** the narrations differ on the final word. In the editions I checked:
  - **Abu Dawud 5068:** morning ends "wa ilaykan-nushur". Evening is "Allahumma bika amsayna wa bika nahya wa bika namutu wa ilaykan-nushur" (*nushur* in both, and the evening has no "wa bika asbahna").
  - **Tirmidhi 3391:** morning ends "wa ilaykal-masir". Evening is "Allahumma bika amsayna wa bika asbahna wa bika nahya wa bika namutu wa ilaykan-nushur". That is word for word the user's evening wording.
  - **Al-Adab al-Mufrad 1199:** morning *nushur*, evening *masir* (per Dorar). This is the split most Hisn al-Muslim editions print, so it is the "common" wording the user's sheet differs from.
  - **Ibn Majah 3868:** the evening ends "wa ilaykal-masir". The copy I checked showed the morning ending at "wa bika namut".
  - Al-Baghawi's *Sharh al-Sunnah* has *masir* in both (per Dorar).
  - So the user's *nushur* in both sets has direct support in Abu Dawud 5068, and its evening wording matches Tirmidhi 3391 exactly. This is information only, since the user has already decided to keep the wording.
- ***an-nushur* vs *al-masir*:** *nushur* is the raising up after death (resurrection). *Masir* is the final return or destination. Translators mostly render *nushur* as "the resurrection" (Sunnah.com Tirmidhi: "to You is the Resurrection"; Darussalam: "and to You is the resurrection"). In one passage Ahmad Hasan's Abu Dawud renders it with a verb ("to Thee are we resurrected"). I kept the noun.
- ***bika*:** the *ba'* of seeking help and accompaniment: "by You", that is, by Your power, will and favour. Some render it "with Your help". I kept "by You", which both compared translations use.
- The tense of *asbahna* (past): both translations render it as "we enter the morning". I followed them, since the dhikr is said as the morning begins.

## 4. Sayyid al-Istighfar (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ خَلَقْتَنِي وَأَنَا عَبْدُكَ وَأَنَا عَلَى عَهْدِكَ وَوَعْدِكَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُ أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا صَنَعْتُ أَبُوءُ لَكَ بِنِعْمَتِكَ عَلَيَّ وَأَبُوءُ لَكَ بِذَنْبِي فَاغْفِرْ لِي فَإِنَّهُ لَا يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma anta Rabbi, la ilaha illa anta, khalaqtani wa ana 'abduka, wa ana 'ala 'ahdika wa wa'dika mastata'tu, a'udhu bika min sharri ma sana'tu, abu'u laka bi-ni'matika 'alayya, wa abu'u laka bi-dhanbi, faghfir li, fa-innahu la yaghfirudh-dhunuba illa ant.

**Translation:** O Allah, You are my Lord; there is no god but You. You created me and I am Your servant, and I am upon Your covenant and Your promise as far as I am able. I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done. I acknowledge before You Your favour upon me, and I acknowledge before You my sin. So forgive me, for none forgives sins except You.

**Source:** Sahih al-Bukhari 6306, from Shaddad ibn Aws. The text I checked reads "أَبُوءُ لَكَ بِنِعْمَتِكَ عَلَيَّ وَأَبُوءُ لَكَ بِذَنْبِي", the same as the user's.

**Virtue:** It is the master of seeking forgiveness. Whoever says it during the day with firm conviction and dies that day before evening is among the people of Paradise. Whoever says it at night with firm conviction and dies before morning is among the people of Paradise (Bukhari 6306).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- ***abu'u***: literally "I return bearing" or "I come back with". Commentators (Ibn Hajar, *Fath al-Bari*) explain it as *uqirru wa a'tarifu*, "I affirm and confess". Translators vary:
  - "I acknowledge" (Darussalam Hisn al-Muslim);
  - "I acknowledge ... and I confess" (Muhsin Khan, with a different verb for each half);
  - "I return to You with...", the literal sense.
  I used "acknowledge" for both, because the Arabic repeats the same verb. *laka* is rendered "before You".
- ***'ala 'ahdika wa wa'dika***: some take *'ahd* as the covenant Allah took from the servant (tawhid, obedience) and *wa'd* as Allah's promise of reward. Others render it "I keep my covenant and promise to You", which makes both the servant's. I kept the neutral "Your covenant and Your promise", which follows the pronoun in the Arabic.
- ***ma sana'tu***: "what I have done" (some write "what I have committed"). The neutral wording follows the Arabic.
- No count is given in the hadith. Once matches the hadith, which speaks of saying it once in the day and once at night.

## 5. Asbahna 'ala fitratil-Islam (once)

**Arabic:** أَصْبَحْنَا (أَمْسَيْنَا*) عَلَى فِطْرَةِ الْإِسْلَامِ وَعَلَى كَلِمَةِ الْإِخْلَاصِ وَعَلَى دِينِ نَبِيِّنَا مُحَمَّدٍ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ وَعَلَى مِلَّةِ أَبِينَا إِبْرَاهِيمَ حَنِيفًا مُسْلِمًا وَمَا كَانَ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration, morning:** Asbahna 'ala fitratil-Islami, wa 'ala kalimatil-ikhlasi, wa 'ala dini nabiyyina Muhammadin sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallama, wa 'ala millati abina Ibrahima hanifan musliman, wa ma kana minal-mushrikin.

**Transliteration, evening:** Amsayna 'ala fitratil-Islami, wa 'ala kalimatil-ikhlasi, wa 'ala dini nabiyyina Muhammadin sallallahu 'alayhi wa sallama, wa 'ala millati abina Ibrahima hanifan musliman, wa ma kana minal-mushrikin.

**Translation, morning:** We have entered the morning upon the natural way (fitrah) of Islam, upon the word of sincere devotion, upon the religion of our Prophet Muhammad (may Allah bless him and grant him peace), and upon the way (millah) of our father Ibrahim, who was upright (hanif), a Muslim, and was not one of the polytheists.

**Translation, evening:** We have entered the evening upon ... (the rest is the same).

**Source:**
- Musnad Ahmad, from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abza. The hadith number is **[unverified]**; it is commonly cited as 15360 or 15367.
- 'Abdullah ibn Ahmad, *Zawa'id al-Musnad* 21144 (from Ubayy ibn Ka'b) and al-Tabarani, *al-Du'a* 293. These are reported by Dorar and surahquran.com: **[unverified in the primary text]**.

**Virtue:** none stated in the hadith.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- ***kalimat al-ikhlas***: the commentators explain it as *la ilaha illallah*. I translated the phrase literally ("the word of sincere devotion") and added no gloss. Some translators write "the word of pure faith" or add "(la ilaha illallah)" in brackets.
- ***hanifan musliman***: both describe Ibrahim (a *hal*), not the speaker. "Wa ma kana minal-mushrikin" also refers to Ibrahim, echoing Al 'Imran 3:67. The translation keeps that reference.
- **The wording:** narrations differ slightly. Some read "وَكَلِمَةِ الْإِخْلَاصِ" and "وَدِينِ نَبِيِّنَا" without the repeated "عَلَى". Some printed versions include "صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ" and others leave it out. The user's wording, with "'ala" repeated and the salawat, matches several of the versions Dorar lists. Information only.
- No count is given in the hadith, so once is fine.

## 6. Allahumma ma asbaha bi min ni'matin (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ مَا أَصْبَحَ (مَا أَمْسَى*) بِي مِنْ نِعْمَةٍ، أَوْ بِأَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، فَمِنْكَ وَحْدَكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ، فَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ وَلَكَ الشُّكْرُ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration, morning:** Allahumma ma asbaha bi min ni'matin, aw bi-ahadin min khalqika, fa-minka wahdaka la sharika laka, fa-lakal-hamdu wa lakash-shukr.

**Transliteration, evening:** Allahumma ma amsa bi min ni'matin, aw bi-ahadin min khalqika, fa-minka wahdaka la sharika laka, fa-lakal-hamdu wa lakash-shukr.

**Translation, morning:** O Allah, whatever blessing I or any of Your creation have this morning is from You alone; You have no partner. So to You belongs all praise and to You belongs all thanks.

**Translation, evening:** O Allah, whatever blessing I or any of Your creation have this evening is from You alone; ... (the rest is the same).

**Source:**
- Abu Dawud 5073, from 'Abdullah ibn Ghannam al-Bayadi. This narration does **not** contain "أَوْ بِأَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ".
- The addition "أَوْ بِأَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ" is in al-Nasa'i, *al-Sunan al-Kubra* (Dorar gives 9835, hadithanswers.com gives 9750; the numbering differs by edition) and Ibn Hibban 861 (one chain names Ibn 'Abbas). These are secondary reports: **[unverified in the primary text]**.

**Virtue:** Whoever says it in the morning has fulfilled the thanks due for his day, and whoever says it in the evening has fulfilled the thanks due for his night (Abu Dawud 5073).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **Literal structure:** *ma asbaha bi min ni'matin* is literally "whatever of blessing has come into the morning with me". Darussalam has "Whatever blessing has been received by me or anyone of Your creation". I rendered it "whatever blessing I or any of Your creation have this morning", which keeps both the "whatever" and the time.
- The user's wording includes "aw bi-ahadin min khalqika", which follows the Nasa'i and Ibn Hibban narrations, not Abu Dawud's.
- No count is given in the hadith, so once is fine.

## 7. Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazan (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ، وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ غَلَبَةِ الدَّيْنِ، وَقَهْرِ الرِّجَالِ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazani, wa a'udhu bika minal-'ajzi wal-kasali, wa a'udhu bika minal-jubni wal-bukhli, wa a'udhu bika min ghalabatid-dayni, wa qahrir-rijal.

**Translation:** O Allah, I seek refuge in You from worry and grief. I seek refuge in You from incapacity and laziness. I seek refuge in You from cowardice and miserliness. And I seek refuge in You from being overwhelmed by debt and from being subdued by men.

**Source:** Abu Dawud 1555, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. In this hadith the Prophet ﷺ taught it to Abu Umamah, to be said "when you enter the morning and when you enter the evening". A close wording, with no stated time, is in Sahih al-Bukhari 6369 (Anas: the Prophet ﷺ used to say it). That version ends "وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ".

**Virtue:** Abu Umamah said that when he did this, Allah removed his worry and settled his debt (Abu Dawud 1555).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- The user's wording ("غَلَبَةِ الدَّيْنِ وَقَهْرِ الرِّجَالِ", with "أَعُوذُ بِكَ" repeated) matches Abu Dawud 1555, which is the narration tied to morning and evening. Bukhari 6369 has no stated time and ends "ضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ" ("the burden of debt and being overpowered by men").
- ***al-hamm* / *al-hazan***: many commentators distinguish *hamm* (anxiety about what is to come) from *hazan* (grief over what has happened). I kept the plain pair "worry and grief", as both compared translations do.
- ***al-'ajz***: "incapacity" or "inability" (Darussalam: "incapacity"; others: "helplessness").
- ***qahr ar-rijal***: "being subdued, dominated or oppressed by men". Renderings range from "the oppression of men" to "being overpowered by men".
- No count is given in the hadith, so once is fine.

## 8. Allahumma anta Rabbi ... 'alayka tawakkaltu (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ عَلَيْكَ تَوَكَّلْتُ، وَأَنْتَ رَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْكَرِيمِ، مَا شَاءَ اللهُ كَانَ وَمَا لَمْ يَشَأْ لَمْ يَكُنْ، لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللهِ، أَعْلَمُ أَنَّ اللهَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ وَأَنَّ اللهَ قَدْ أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا، اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ نَفْسِي وَمِنْ شَرِّ كُلِّ دَابَّةٍ أَنْتَ آخِذٌ بِنَاصِيَتِهَا إِنَّ رَبِّي عَلَى صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma anta Rabbi, la ilaha illa anta, 'alayka tawakkaltu, wa anta Rabbul-'Arshil-Karimi, ma sha'allahu kana, wa ma lam yasha' lam yakun, la hawla wa la quwwata illa billahi, a'lamu annallaha 'ala kulli shay'in qadirun, wa annallaha qad ahata bi-kulli shay'in 'ilman, Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min sharri nafsi, wa min sharri kulli dabbatin anta akhidhun bi-nasiyatiha, inna Rabbi 'ala siratin mustaqim.

**Translation:** O Allah, You are my Lord; there is no god but You. In You I have put my trust, and You are the Lord of the Noble Throne. What Allah wills happens, and what He does not will does not happen. There is no power and no strength except through Allah. I know that Allah has power over all things, and that Allah encompasses all things in knowledge. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the evil of my own self, and from the evil of every creature whose forelock You hold. Indeed, my Lord is on a straight path.

**Source:** from Abu al-Darda'.
- Ibn al-Sunni, *'Amal al-Yawm wa al-Laylah*, through Talq ibn Habib, in the story of Abu al-Darda's house being spared from a fire. Al-Nawawi quotes it in *al-Adhkar* (no. 239 in one edition). The hadith number in Ibn al-Sunni is **[unverified]**.
- Also attributed to al-Tabarani (*al-Du'a*) and al-Bayhaqi: **[unverified]**.

**Virtue:** Whoever says it at the start of the day, no calamity befalls him until evening, and whoever says it at the end of the day, none befalls him until morning (reported in the Abu al-Darda' narration; secondary source only, so **[unverified]**).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **The user's wording differs from the narrations I could find.** In the texts quoted by surahquran.com and mawdoo3.com:
  - it is **"رَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْعَظِيمِ"** (the Mighty Throne), where the user has "الْكَرِيمِ";
  - after "عِلْمًا" it adds **"وَأَحْصَى كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عَدَدًا"**;
  - after "شَرِّ نَفْسِي" it adds **"وَمِنْ شَرِّ الشَّيْطَانِ وَشَرَكِهِ"**.
  Versions vary between books, and the user may be following a specific printed source. Stated for information only. "Rabbul-'Arshil-Karim" is itself Qur'anic (al-Mu'minun 23:116).
- **Qur'anic phrases:** "anta akhidhun bi-nasiyatiha ... inna Rabbi 'ala siratin mustaqim" echoes Hud 11:56 ("ma min dabbatin illa huwa akhidhun bi-nasiyatiha"). "Ahata bi-kulli shay'in 'ilman" echoes al-Talaq 65:12.
- ***dabbah***: any creature that moves on the earth. I used "creature", as most translators do of Hud 11:56.
- ***akhidhun bi-nasiyatiha***: literally "holding its forelock", meaning it is wholly under Your control. I kept the literal image.
- This dhikr is not in Hisn al-Muslim, so I compared only Sunnah.com-style renderings of the Qur'anic phrases and my own reading.
- I could not reach a primary text of this narration, so the scholar may want to confirm which source the user's sheet relies on.

## 9. Allahumma 'afini fi badani (3 times)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ عَافِنِي فِي بَدَنِي، اللَّهُمَّ عَافِنِي فِي سَمْعِي، اللَّهُمَّ عَافِنِي فِي بَصَرِي، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma 'afini fi badani, Allahumma 'afini fi sam'i, Allahumma 'afini fi basari, la ilaha illa ant.

**Translation:** O Allah, grant me well-being in my body. O Allah, grant me well-being in my hearing. O Allah, grant me well-being in my sight. There is no god but You.

**Source:** Abu Dawud 5090, from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakrah, from his father Abu Bakrah: he said it "three times in the morning and three times in the evening" and said he heard the Prophet ﷺ supplicate with these words. Also Musnad Ahmad (20430 in one numbering) **[unverified]**.

**Virtue:** none stated in the hadith.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- ***'afini***: "grant me *'afiyah*", which means soundness and health and also protection from harm and affliction. Darussalam has "make me healthy in my body"; others write "grant me well-being" or "grant me health". I chose "well-being" because it covers both senses.
- The count of 3 matches the hadith.

## 10. Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-kufri wal-faqr (3 times)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْكُفْرِ، وَالْفَقْرِ، اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ عَذَابِ الْقَبْرِ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-kufri wal-faqri, Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min 'adhabil-qabri, la ilaha illa ant.

**Translation:** O Allah, I seek refuge in You from disbelief and poverty. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the punishment of the grave. There is no god but You.

**Source:** Abu Dawud 5090, the same hadith as #9. This part comes in the narration of Abu Dawud's teacher al-'Abbas ("قَالَ عَبَّاسٌ فِيهِ"), within Abu Bakrah's supplication, which he repeated three times morning and evening.

**Virtue:** none stated in the hadith.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- In Abu Dawud 5090 the text of #9 and #10 forms one supplication (in al-'Abbas's version), and the "three times" applies to it. The user's sheet splits it into two items with 3 each, which matches the count.
- The user's sheet has a comma between "al-kufr" and "wal-faqr". It does not change the meaning.
- ***'adhab al-qabr***: "the punishment of the grave" (both compared translations).

## 11. Allahumma rahmataka arju (once)

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ رَحْمَتَكَ أَرْجُو، فَلَا تَكِلْنِي إِلَى نَفْسِي طَرْفَةَ عَيْنٍ، وَأَصْلِحْ لِي شَأْنِي كُلَّهُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

**Count:** 1

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Allahumma rahmataka arju, fa-la takilni ila nafsi tarfata 'aynin, wa aslih li sha'ni kullahu, la ilaha illa ant.

**Translation:** O Allah, it is Your mercy that I hope for, so do not leave me to myself even for the blink of an eye, and set right all my affairs for me. There is no god but You.

**Source:** Abu Dawud 5090, the same hadith as #9 and #10. Here the Prophet ﷺ calls these words "دَعَوَاتُ الْمَكْرُوبِ", the supplications of one in distress. Also Musnad Ahmad 20430 and Ibn Hibban 970 **[unverified]**.

**Virtue:** none stated in the hadith, beyond its description as the supplication of the distressed.

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **The context in the hadith:** this part is introduced as the supplication of the distressed. It is not tied to morning and evening, unlike #9 and #10 in the same hadith. Hisn al-Muslim places it under supplications for distress, not under the morning and evening adhkar. Some later collections of morning and evening adhkar do include it. Stated for information only.
- ***rahmataka arju***: the object comes first, which in Arabic rhetoric gives a sense of restriction ("Your mercy [alone] I hope for"). I wrote "it is Your mercy that I hope for" to carry that emphasis without adding words.
- ***tarfata 'aynin***: "the blink of an eye" (or "a glance"). Translators use either.
- No count is given in the hadith, so once is fine.

## 12. Subhanallahi wa bi-hamdihi 'adada khalqihi (3 times)

**Arabic:** سُبْحَانَ اللهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ، عَدَدَ خَلْقِهِ وَرِضَا نَفْسِهِ وَزِنَةَ عَرْشِهِ وَمِدَادَ كَلِمَاتِهِ

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** Subhanallahi wa bi-hamdihi, 'adada khalqihi, wa rida nafsihi, wa zinata 'arshihi, wa midada kalimatih.

**Translation:** Glory be to Allah and praise be to Him, as many times as the number of His creation, as much as pleases Him, as much as the weight of His Throne, and as much as the ink of His words.

**Source:** Sahih Muslim 2726, from Juwayriyah bint al-Harith. The Prophet ﷺ left her after the Fajr prayer and came back in the forenoon (duha) to find her still in her place of prayer. He said he had said four phrases three times after leaving her.

**Virtue:** If these words were weighed against everything she had said since the morning, they would outweigh it (Muslim 2726).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **The time:** in the hadith these words come in the morning, after Fajr, and the virtue is measured against what she said "since the morning". Hisn al-Muslim lists it for the morning only. The user's sheet uses it in both sets. Stated for information only.
- ***rida nafsihi***: literally "the pleasure of His Self". I wrote "as much as pleases Him". Others write "as much as His pleasure" or "to His satisfaction".
- ***midada kalimatihi***: *midad* is ink, or the supply that extends something. Both compared translations give "the ink of His words".
- The count of 3 matches the hadith.

## 13. A'udhu bi-kalimatillahit-tammati (3 times)

**Arabic:** أَعُوذُ بِكَلِمَاتِ اللهِ التَّامَّاتِ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ

**Count:** 3

**Transliteration (morning and evening):** A'udhu bi-kalimatillahit-tammati min sharri ma khalaq.

**Translation:** I seek refuge in the perfect words of Allah from the evil of what He has created.

**Source:**
- Sahih Muslim 2708, from Khawlah bint Hakim: said on stopping at a place.
- Sahih Muslim 2709, from Abu Hurayrah: a man was stung by a scorpion, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "Had you said, when you entered the evening, ...".
- Tirmidhi 3604, from Abu Hurayrah: three times in the evening.
- Muslim's numbering differs between editions (2708/2709 in Fu'ad 'Abd al-Baqi's numbering; sub-numbers differ between sites).

**Virtue:** Whoever says it three times when evening comes, no venomous sting will harm him that night (Tirmidhi 3604). Whoever says it on stopping at a place, nothing will harm him until he leaves that place (Muslim 2708).

**Notes for the scholar:**
- **The time:** the narrations tie it to the **evening** (Muslim 2709, Tirmidhi 3604). Hisn al-Muslim lists it for the evening only. The user's sheet uses it in both sets. Stated for information only.
- **The count:** 3 comes from Tirmidhi 3604 (evening). Muslim gives no count.
- ***kalimat Allah at-tammat***: "the perfect (complete) words of Allah". Commentators (e.g. al-Nawawi) explain "tammat" as free of any flaw or deficiency. Some take "kalimat" as the Qur'an, others as His words generally. I kept "perfect words", as both compared translations do.
- ***ma khalaq***: "what He has created". Some translators write "all that He has created".

## Second-pass check (transliteration against harakat, translation against the Arabic)

I checked every syllable of each transliteration against the harakat, and every translation against the Arabic word by word. Corrections made in that pass:

**Transliteration**
1. I had mixed two styles: dropping endings at every comma, and writing full connected endings. I unified all 13 on one rule: full connected endings, with a pause form only on the final word. This changed #4 ("'abduka", "mastata'tu"), #5 ("fitratil-Islami", "kalimatil-ikhlasi", "sallama"), #7 ("wal-hazani", "wal-kasali", "wal-bukhli"), #8 ("Karimi", "billahi", "qadirun", "'ilman"), #10 ("wal-faqri", "'adhabil-qabri"), #11 ("'aynin", "kullahu") and #12 ("bi-hamdihi", "khalqihi", "nafsihi", "'arshihi").
2. In #8, I removed the tajwid assimilations I had first written ("akhidhum-bi", "siratim-mustaqim") and replaced them with "akhidhun bi-" and "siratin mustaqim", to match the no-assimilation rule used elsewhere.
3. In #4, I fixed "ma istata'tu" to "mastata'tu" (the hamzat al-wasl of اسْتَطَعْتُ is silent).
4. In #13, I fixed "bi kalimatillahit-tammat" to "bi-kalimatillahit-tammati" (the word continues into "min", so the kasrah is pronounced).
5. In #5, I fixed "Ibrahim hanifan" to "Ibrahima hanifan" (إِبْرَاهِيمَ is diptote, with a fathah).
6. In #3 and #10, I made the hyphens and article forms consistent ("ilaykan-nushur", "minal-kufri").

**Translation**
1. In #2, I changed "Messenger" to "**Prophet**" to match the user's نَبِيًّا. "Messenger" belongs to Abu Dawud's رَسُولًا.
2. In #1, I changed "the heavens" to "**the heaven**", because السَّمَاءِ is singular.
3. In #3, I translated النُّشُورُ consistently as "**the resurrection**", so it stays distinct from المصير ("the final return"), which the user's text does not use.
4. In #4, I used the same verb ("acknowledge") for both occurrences of أَبُوءُ, because the Arabic repeats one verb. I also kept "Your covenant and Your promise" to follow the pronoun كَ.
5. In #6, I reworked the opening so it keeps both "whatever (ما) ... blessing" and the morning or evening time. I removed "upon me", which was not in the Arabic.
6. In #8, I changed "Mighty Throne", carried over from the common narration, to "**Noble Throne**", to match the user's الْكَرِيمِ.
7. In #11, I removed an inserted "alone" and expressed the emphasis of the fronted object through word order ("it is Your mercy that I hope for").
8. In #12, I removed "His good pleasure" for رِضَا نَفْسِهِ, which over-interpreted it, and used "as much as pleases Him".
