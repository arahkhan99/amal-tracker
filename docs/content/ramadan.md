# Ramadan content (R-D duʿās, R-H daily cards, R-E Eid cards)

For scholar review. Spec for where these appear: `docs/plan/ramadan.md`.

Sources, as in `BRIEF.md`:
- Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English, scan). In the fasting and night-prayer chapters used here, printed page = PDF page − 1 (checked on every page cited).
- Maʿārif al-Hadīth vol. 1 and vol. 3 (text). Printed page numbers are taken from the page header.
- Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers) (text). Printed page = PDF page in the range used.
- Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged) (text). Printed page = PDF page − 30.
- Ascent to Felicity is cited only in Notes, for the fiqh points.

Rules followed: no gradings (the collection is named as the book gives it), our own English, nothing added from memory. **Arabic** is given only for the duʿās. It is copied from the reviewed set in `dua.md`, which was read from the Maʿārif al-Hadīth scan with the user's approval. The daily cards have no Arabic, because Riyād al-Sālihīn is a scan and Zād al-Tālibīn's Arabic comes out garbled.

Transliteration follows `dua.md`: simple, with no macrons or dots.

Note on Maʿārif al-Hadīth: its chapter on fasting (Kitāb al-Ṣawm) is in **volume 4**, which we don't have. Vol. 3, p. 215 says so itself: "these will be found in volume 4 … Book of Fasting". That is the main reason two of the ifṭār duʿās could not be found (see R-D02, R-D03).

## R-D: the necessary Ramadan duʿās (6)

### R-D01
- **Title:** On seeing the new crescent
- **Arabic:** اَللّٰهُمَّ اَهِلَّهٗ عَلَيْنَا بِالْاَمْنِ وَالْاِيْمَانِ وَالسَّلَامَةِ وَالْاِسْلَامِ رَبِّىْ وَرَبُّكَ اللهُ
- **Transliteration:** Allahumma ahillahu 'alayna bil-amni wal-iman, was-salamati wal-islam. Rabbi wa rabbukallah.
- **Text:** O Allah, let this new moon rise over us with safety and faith, with peace and Islam. (O moon,) my Lord and your Lord is Allah.
- **Narrator:** Talhah ibn ʿUbaydullāh
- **Collection:** Tirmizi
- **Source:** Maʿārif al-Hadīth, vol. 3, no. 1244/201, pp. 213–214 (pdf pp. 202–203)
- **Note:** Same as D43 in `dua.md` (already reviewed); the Arabic is copied from there unchanged. The same open point applies: the sign on اَهِلَّهٗ. D44 ("Hilalu khayrin wa rushd …", pp. 214–215) is the book's other duʿāʾ for the new moon, if the scholar prefers it or wants both. It isn't specific to Ramadan. Riyād al-Sālihīn no. 661, p. 802 (pdf p. 803) supports the timing: "Start fasting on seeing the crescent" (Bukhari).

### R-D02
- **Title:** At ifṭār: the thirst is gone
- **Arabic:** (none: no approved source to copy it from)
- **Transliteration:** Dhahabaz-zama'u wabtallatil-'uruqu, wa thabatal-ajru in sha' Allah.
- **Text:** The thirst is gone, the veins are moist, and the reward is certain, if Allah wills.
- **Narrator:** ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar (as the primary source gives it)
- **Collection:** Abu Dawud
- **Source:** NOT FOUND in approved books. Standard primary reference: Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Kitāb al-Ṣawm, "what to say at ifṭār" (no. 2357 in the common numbering).
- **Note:** NOT FOUND in approved books — needs scholar decision. Searched: Maʿārif al-Hadīth vols. 1 and 3 (text), Zād al-Tālibīn (text), Riyād al-Sālihīn fasting chapters 201–212 and Laylat al-Qadr / night prayer chapters 197–198 (scan pp. 755–824), Seeratul Mustafa, Ascent to Felicity. The transliteration and meaning are the commonly known wording, written from memory, so the scholar must check them against a printed text. No Arabic will be added until a source is approved. Something the approved books do say: Maʿārif al-Hadīth vol. 3, p. 127 (pdf p. 117) lists "the time of breaking the fast" among the times when duʿāʾ is answered. A card could say that even if no fixed wording is approved.

### R-D03
- **Title:** At ifṭār: for You I fasted
- **Arabic:** (none: no approved source to copy it from)
- **Transliteration:** Allahumma laka sumtu wa 'ala rizqika aftartu.
- **Text:** O Allah, for You I fasted, and with Your provision I break my fast.
- **Narrator:** Muʿādh ibn Zuhrah, who reports that it reached him that the Prophet ﷺ said this (as the primary source gives it)
- **Collection:** Abu Dawud
- **Source:** NOT FOUND in approved books. Standard primary reference: Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Kitāb al-Ṣawm (no. 2358 in the common numbering).
- **Note:** NOT FOUND in approved books — needs scholar decision. The same search as R-D02 was done. This is the version most people in South Asia know. Longer versions with extra phrases (for example "wa bika amantu wa 'alayka tawakkaltu") are widespread, but we have no approved source for any of them. The scholar should choose one version or leave them out. The transliteration is written from memory.

### R-D04
- **Title:** When breaking the fast with others
- **Arabic:** اَفْطَرَ عِنْدَكُمُ الصَّائِمُوْنَ وَاَكَلَ طَعَامَكُمُ الْاَبْرَارُ وَصَلَّتْ عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ
- **Transliteration:** Aftara 'indakumus-sa'imun, wa akala ta'amakumul-abrar, wa sallat 'alaykumul-mala'ikah.
- **Text:** May those who fast break their fast with you, may the righteous eat your food, and may the angels pray for you.
- **Narrator:** Anas
- **Collection:** Abu Dawood
- **Source:** Maʿārif al-Hadīth, vol. 3, no. 1202/159, pp. 182–183 (pdf pp. 171–172)
- **Note:** Same as D18 in `dua.md` (already reviewed); the Arabic is copied unchanged. The book's context: the Prophet ﷺ said it to his host, Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah, after eating bread and oil at his home. The book presents it as a guest's duʿāʾ for the host, and it is commonly said when someone has given you ifṭār. The scholar should confirm that using it for ifṭār is fine to state, for example as "a duʿāʾ for whoever gives you ifṭār".

### R-D05
- **Title:** Laylat al-Qadr
- **Arabic:** اَللّٰهُمَّ اِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّىْ
- **Transliteration:** Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni.
- **Text:** O Allah, You are the Pardoner, and You love to pardon, so pardon me.
- **Narrator:** ʿĀʾishah
- **Collection:** Tirmizi
- **Source:** Maʿārif al-Hadīth, vol. 3, no. 1246/203, p. 215 (pdf p. 204); also Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 640, pp. 763–764 (pdf pp. 764–765)
- **Note:** Same as D45 in `dua.md` (already reviewed); the Arabic is copied from there. Riyād al-Sālihīn's Arabic for the duʿāʾ (in its scan) has the same words: عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي. ʿĀʾishah asked what to say if she knew which night it was, and the Prophet ﷺ taught her this. Riyād cites it as reported by "the Five except Abu Dawood". Some recite it with an added "karīm" after ʿafuwwun. Neither approved book has that word, so it is left out.

### R-D06
- **Title:** The intention for the fast (at suhoor)
- **Arabic:** (none)
- **Transliteration:** (none proposed; see Note)
- **Text:** The intention is in the heart: you mean to fast tomorrow for Allah. Getting up for suhoor with that purpose is itself an intention.
- **Source:** Ascent to Felicity, "The Intention of Fasting", p. 129 (pdf p. 124): every fast needs an intention. For the Ramadan fast it may be made any time from the night before (after Maghrib) until before al-ḍaḥwa al-kubrā the next day. On prayer, the same book (p. 75, pdf p. 70) says "the intention, the place of which is in the heart". The suhoor itself: Riyād al-Sālihīn no. 669, p. 807 (pdf p. 808).
- **Note:** As instructed: the intention is in the heart. The common spoken formula ("wa bi sawmi ghadin nawaytu min shahri ramadan") is **not from a hadith**, and none of the approved books has it. The scholar decides whether to show it at all, perhaps as "a common phrase some say; not required". Until then the app shows only the plain line in Text. Sensitive point: Ascent to Felicity says that for a *make-up* (qaḍāʾ) fast the intention must be made the night before, before Fajr, and must name the fast as a make-up. The make-up fasts screen could add a one-line reminder of that, if the scholar wants.

## R-H: 30 daily Ramadan cards

One card per day of Ramadan, with day 1 at the top. Nothing repeats an item in `hadith.md` or `seerah.md`. Where an item is close to an existing card, the Note says so. The order follows the month: beginnings (1–3), the worth of fasting (4–7), suhoor and ifṭār (8–11), guarding the fast (12–15), generosity (16–19), the night prayer and the last ten nights (20–27), closing the month (28–30).

Counts per book: Riyād al-Sālihīn 20, Zād al-Tālibīn 5, Maʿārif al-Hadīth 2 (vol. 1: 1, vol. 3: 1), Seeratul Mustafa 2, plus one Riyād item from the book's own chapter introduction (R-H04). **Total: 30.**

### R-H01
- **Title:** Begin with the crescent
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: start fasting when you see the new moon, and end the fast when you see it again. If it is hidden by cloud, complete thirty days of Shaʿbān.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 661, p. 802 (pdf p. 803)
- **Note:** The book gives the same hadith again as no. 666, p. 805 (pdf p. 806). This fits the app's Hijri source setting (calculation or a moon-sighting committee). The card shouldn't suggest that the app's date settles the matter; the spec has the app say "follow your local masjid or committee".

### R-H02
- **Title:** The first night
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: on the first night of Ramadan the devils and the rebellious jinn are chained. The gates of the Fire are shut, and not one is opened. The gates of Paradise are opened, and not one is shut. A caller cries: "You who seek good, come forward. You who seek evil, hold back." And every night Allah frees people from the Fire.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 662, pp. 802–803 (pdf pp. 803–804)

### R-H03
- **Title:** Fasting with faith
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever fasts Ramadan with faith, hoping for Allah's reward, has his past sins forgiven.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 660, p. 802 (pdf p. 803)
- **Note:** The app already has this hadith in `src/reflections.js` (cited "Sahih al-Bukhari 38"). It is not in `hadith.md` or `seerah.md`. If both stay, the old reflection should be dropped or re-cited during Ramadan so it doesn't appear twice. The old citation also contains the word "Sahih", which may break the no-grading rule; please confirm. Zād al-Tālibīn no. 236, p. 103 has the same hadith together with the Laylat al-Qadr part (see R-H25).

### R-H04
- **Title:** Half of patience
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: fasting is half of patience. The book explains that hunger and desire are two of the strongest pulls on a person, and fasting trains the self against both.
- **Narrator:** A man of the Banū Sulaym
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), introduction to chapter 201, p. 791 (pdf p. 792)
- **Note:** This comes from the editor's introduction to the chapter, not from Imam al-Nawawī's numbered hadith. The hadith is quoted there in part (with "…"). Please confirm whether a hadith quoted only in the introduction may be used.

### R-H05
- **Title:** A shield
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: fasting is a shield. So the one fasting should keep away from indecent talk and foolish behaviour. If someone fights with him or insults him, let him say twice, "I am fasting."
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 655, pp. 799–800 (pdf pp. 800–801)
- **Note:** Also no. 676, p. 811. The book's English adds "(from Hellfire)" in brackets. We kept just "a shield".

### R-H06
- **Title:** "Fasting is for Me"
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah says: every deed of the son of Ādam is for himself except fasting; it is for Me, and I Myself will reward it. And the one who fasts has two joys: one when he breaks his fast, and one when he meets his Lord.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 656, p. 800 (pdf p. 801)
- **Note:** No. 655, on the same page, adds that the smell of a fasting person's mouth is better with Allah than musk, and that he left his food, drink and desires for Allah's sake. Those lines could be added if the card has room.

### R-H07
- **Title:** The gate called al-Rayyān
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: Paradise has a gate called al-Rayyān. On the Day of Resurrection those who fasted will enter by it, and no one else. It will be called out, "Where are those who fasted?" They will stand and go in, and when they have entered it will be closed.
- **Narrator:** Sahl ibn Saʿd
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 658, p. 801 (pdf p. 802)
- **Note:** No. 657 (pp. 800–801) is a reserve. It tells of people called from the gate of their best deed, and Abū Bakr asking if anyone is called from every gate ("I hope you will be one of them"). It was left out to avoid two "gate" cards.

### R-H08
- **Title:** Blessing in suhoor
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: eat suhoor, for there is blessing in it.
- **Narrator:** Anas ibn Mālik
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 669, p. 807 (pdf p. 808)
- **Note:** The book's footnote says that suhoor is recommended even if it is only a sip of water. Ascent to Felicity, p. 133 (pdf p. 128) says the same.

### R-H09
- **Title:** Suhoor close to Fajr
- **Text:** Zayd ibn Thābit said: we ate suhoor with the Prophet ﷺ, then he stood for the prayer. He was asked how long there was between the suhoor and the adhān. He said: about as long as it takes to recite fifty verses.
- **Narrator:** Anas, from Zayd ibn Thābit
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 670, p. 807 (pdf p. 808)
- **Note:** This matches the Hanafi recommendation to delay suhoor until shortly before Fajr while being sure to stop in time (Ascent to Felicity, p. 133). In the app, "suhoor ends" is the Fajr time (see the spec), with a margin for safety.

### R-H10
- **Title:** Do not delay ifṭār
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: people will stay in good as long as they hurry to break the fast. In another hadith, Allah says: the dearest of My servants to Me are those quickest to break their fast.
- **Narrator:** Sahl ibn Saʿd; and Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari; and Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), nos. 672–673, p. 809 (pdf p. 810)
- **Note:** Two short hadith on one theme. Ascent to Felicity, p. 133 adds the condition: hurrying is recommended once Maghrib has surely come in, but not on a cloudy day when you can't be sure. The app's ifṭār time is the calculated Maghrib time, so a "check your local timetable" line would fit.

### R-H11
- **Title:** Dates, then water
- **Text:** Anas said: the Prophet ﷺ used to break his fast with a few fresh dates before he prayed. If there were none, then with a few dried dates. If there were none, he took a few sips of water.
- **Narrator:** Anas ibn Mālik
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 675, p. 810 (pdf p. 811)
- **Note:** No. 674 on the same page is the Prophet's ﷺ instruction: break the fast with dates, and if you find none, with water, "for it is purifying". It could be added in one line.

### R-H12
- **Title:** Leave false speech
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever does not give up false speech and acting on it, Allah has no need of his giving up food and drink. The book explains that fasting is meant to purify the self, not only to empty the stomach. It names the sins of the tongue: lying, false witness, cursing, slander, backbiting and abuse.
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers), no. 219, p. 97 (pdf p. 97)
- **Note:** The same hadith is in Riyād al-Sālihīn no. 677, pp. 811–812. Zād was chosen because its commentary lists the sins.

### R-H13
- **Title:** More than hunger
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: how many fast and gain nothing from it but hunger, and how many stand at night in prayer and gain nothing but lost sleep. The book explains that this means fasting without seeking Allah's reward, or without giving up sins, even though the duty itself is fulfilled.
- **Collection:** Darimi
- **Source:** Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers), no. 59, p. 46 (pdf p. 46)
- **Note:** Riyād al-Sālihīn p. 795 quotes a shorter form, citing Ibn Majah (in its introduction, not a numbered hadith). We use Zād's form. The tone is a warning, so it is placed after the encouraging cards.

### R-H14
- **Title:** If you forget and eat
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: if someone forgets and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast. It was Allah who fed him and gave him drink.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 678, p. 812 (pdf p. 813)
- **Note:** This agrees with the Hanafi ruling that eating out of forgetfulness does not break the fast. The scholar may want a short fiqh line here, or none.

### R-H15
- **Title:** A day that cannot be replaced
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever misses a fast of Ramadan without a valid excuse or illness, even fasting for a lifetime will not make up for it. The book explains that what is lost is the special virtue of a Ramadan fast. The duty itself is still met by fasting a day in its place.
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud
- **Source:** Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers), no. 229, p. 101 (pdf p. 101)
- **Note:** This links to the make-up fasts the app tracks. The card's wording avoids scaring people with a valid excuse (illness, travel, hayd/nifās), as the hadith itself says. Please confirm the tone. The card could be left out of the daily rotation and shown only from the fasts log, when someone marks a day "Not fasting – other".

### R-H16
- **Title:** Give someone their ifṭār
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever gives a fasting person food to break his fast earns the same reward as him, without the fasting person's reward being lessened at all.
- **Narrator:** Zayd ibn Khālid al-Juhanī
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 692, p. 822 (pdf p. 823)
- **Note:** The book's English prints the narrator as "Sa'ad ibn Khaled Al-Johani", but its Arabic has زيد بن خالد الجهني. We followed the Arabic. Zād al-Tālibīn no. 232, p. 102 has a similar hadith from Bayhaqi; it was not used, to avoid repeating.

### R-H17
- **Title:** Badr, in Ramadan
- **Text:** The Battle of Badr was fought on Friday, the seventeenth of Ramadan, in the second year after the Hijrah. Allah called it the Day of Furqān, the day that separated truth from falsehood. ʿAlī said that the night before the battle everyone slept except the Prophet ﷺ, who spent the whole night until morning in salāh and duʿāʾ.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 223, 235 (pdf pp. 253, 265)
- **Note:** Placed on day 17 to match the date. This is near S29 ("The Duʿāʾ at Badr", pp. 240–241). That card is about his duʿāʾ on the battlefield. This one is about the date, the name "Furqān" and his night of prayer before the battle. The book also says (p. 223) that Ramadan itself is "the month of Furqān" because the Qurʾān was revealed in it.

### R-H18
- **Title:** Bilāl's meal in Paradise
- **Text:** Bilāl came to the Prophet ﷺ while he was eating and was offered food. He said, "I am fasting." The Prophet ﷺ said: we are eating our provision, and Bilāl's provision is kept for him in Paradise. Do you know, Bilāl, that a fasting person's bones glorify Allah, and the angels ask forgiveness for him, as long as food is eaten in front of him?
- **Narrator:** Buraydah
- **Collection:** Ibn Maja, Bayhaqi (Shuʿab al-Īmān)
- **Source:** Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers), Part 3, no. 9, p. 137 (pdf p. 137)
- **Note:** Riyād al-Sālihīn no. 693, pp. 822–823 makes a similar point (Umm ʿUmārah: angels pray for a fasting person while others eat in front of him, Tirmidhi). It was not used, to avoid repeating. The story probably concerns a voluntary fast. It suits Ramadan days at work or school, when others are eating.

### R-H19
- **Title:** Most generous in Ramadan
- **Text:** Ibn ʿAbbās said: the Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was at his most generous in Ramadan, when Jibrīl met him. Jibrīl met him every night of Ramadan and went over the Qurʾān with him. At such times the Prophet ﷺ was more generous with good than the free-blowing wind.
- **Narrator:** Ibn ʿAbbās
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 663, pp. 803–804 (pdf pp. 804–805)
- **Note:** The "free-blowing wind" follows the book's Arabic (الرِّيحِ الْمُرْسَلَةِ). Its English paraphrases it as "readiness and haste". This is close to S47, which mentions twenty days of iʿtikāf in his last Ramadan. This card is about generosity and the Qurʾān, so it doesn't repeat it. Fits the khatm planner.

### R-H20
- **Title:** Standing in Ramadan
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever stands in prayer through Ramadan with faith, hoping for Allah's reward, has his past sins forgiven. The book explains that "standing" here means the Tarāwīḥ prayer, or ṭawāf of the Kaʿbah for one in Makkah.
- **Collection:** Bukhari, Muslim
- **Source:** Zād al-Tālibīn (Provisions for the Seekers), no. 237, p. 104 (pdf p. 104)
- **Note:** Placed just before the last ten nights. This is the Tarāwīḥ card, and it fits the Tarāwīḥ tracking.

### R-H21
- **Title:** The last ten begin
- **Text:** ʿĀʾishah said: when the last ten nights of Ramadan began, the Prophet ﷺ tightened his waist-wrapper, kept his nights alive in worship, and woke his family.
- **Narrator:** ʿĀʾishah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 664, p. 804 (pdf p. 805)
- **Note:** The book glosses "tightening the waist-wrapper" in brackets as "to work diligently", meaning getting ready to work hard in worship. No. 639, p. 763 (Tirmidhi) is a reserve: "he strove in the last ten more than at any other time".

### R-H22
- **Title:** Iʿtikāf
- **Text:** ʿĀʾishah said: the Prophet ﷺ used to stay in iʿtikāf in the last ten days of Ramadan until Allah took him, and after him his wives kept up iʿtikāf.
- **Narrator:** ʿĀʾishah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 695, p. 824 (pdf p. 825)
- **Note:** This is the iʿtikāf note. Hanafi point for the scholar: men sit iʿtikāf in the masjid, women in the place set aside for prayer at home (the glossary in Maʿārif al-Hadīth vol. 3, p. 562 says "Men do it in the mosque and women at home"). Ascent to Felicity, pp. 136ff (pdf pp. 131ff) covers the rulings. The app should say only that, and point to a local scholar for details. No. 694, p. 823 (Ibn ʿUmar) is a shorter version.

### R-H23
- **Title:** Seek it in the last ten
- **Text:** ʿĀʾishah said: the Prophet ﷺ used to stay in iʿtikāf in the last ten nights of Ramadan, and he would say, "Seek Laylat al-Qadr in the last ten nights of Ramadan." The book adds that most reports say: seek it in the odd nights of the last ten.
- **Narrator:** ʿĀʾishah
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 636, pp. 760–761 (pdf pp. 761–762)
- **Note:** The "odd nights" line is from Imam al-Tirmidhī's comment, which the book quotes. The comment goes on to name the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, 29th and last nights. Please leave out al-Shāfiʿī's preference (the 21st), which the book also quotes, in keeping with the Hanafi approach. This card is not about fiqh, so it doesn't matter either way.

### R-H24
- **Title:** Pray with the imam to the end
- **Text:** Abū Dharr said: we fasted Ramadan with the Prophet ﷺ. Near the end of the month he led us in night prayer on some nights, once until half the night had passed. We asked him to lead us for the rest of the night. He said: whoever prays with the imam until he finishes has the whole night's prayer written for him. On the 27th he gathered his family and wives and led us so long that we feared we would miss suhoor.
- **Narrator:** Abū Dharr
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 635, pp. 759–760 (pdf pp. 760–761)
- **Note:** Good for Tarāwīḥ in congregation. The book's Arabic includes al-Tirmidhī's note on how many rakʿahs scholars prayed (20 or 41). We say nothing about the count. Shortened from the book's longer account of which nights he led them.

### R-H25
- **Title:** The night of Qadr
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr with faith, hoping for Allah's reward, has his past sins forgiven.
- **Narrator:** Abū Hurayrah
- **Collection:** Bukhari
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 633, p. 756 (pdf p. 757)
- **Note:** The book's footnote 18 says the night is one of the odd nights of the last ten and its exact time is not known. Pair this card with the Laylat al-Qadr duʿāʾ (R-D05).

### R-H26
- **Title:** Seek it on the remaining nights
- **Text:** Abū Bakrah said that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say: seek it when nine nights remain, or seven, or five, or three. In the first twenty days of Ramadan Abū Bakrah prayed as he did the rest of the year, but when the last ten came he worked hard in worship.
- **Narrator:** Abū Bakrah (via ʿUyaynah ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, from his father)
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 638, pp. 762–763 (pdf pp. 763–764)

### R-H27
- **Title:** A sign of the night
- **Text:** Zirr asked Ubayy ibn Kaʿb how he knew Laylat al-Qadr was the twenty-seventh. Ubayy said the Prophet ﷺ told them its sign: the sun rises the next morning without rays. "So we counted and remembered."
- **Narrator:** Zirr, from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 637, p. 762 (pdf p. 763)
- **Note:** Placed on day 27. The card must not say that the 27th is certainly the night. The same report says Ibn Masʿūd knew but did not say so, "lest you rely on that", and the footnote calls the timing unknown. Suggested closing line: "Seek it in every odd night."

### R-H28
- **Title:** Jibrīl's three duʿās
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said "Āmīn" on each of three steps of the minbar. He then explained that Jibrīl had come and said: ruined is the one who reaches Ramadan and is not forgiven. Ruined is the one before whom you are mentioned and who does not send blessings on you. Ruined is the one whose parents, or one of them, reach old age and he does not earn Paradise through them. To each one he said "Āmīn".
- **Narrator:** Kaʿb ibn ʿUjrah
- **Collection:** Haakim
- **Source:** Maʿārif al-Hadīth, vol. 3, no. 1342/299, p. 310 (pdf p. 296)
- **Note:** The book explains that all three are people who let a chance for Allah's mercy slip away. Placed near the end as a gentle push to turn to Allah while days remain; the tone is a warning. A nearby hadith in the book from Abū Hurayrah (no. 1341/298, p. 309, Tirmizi) has the same three. Not in `durud-40.md` (checked).

### R-H29
- **Title:** The doors of goodness
- **Text:** Muʿādh asked for a deed that would take him to Paradise. The Prophet ﷺ named the pillars of Islam, then said: shall I tell you the doors of goodness? Fasting is a shield. Charity puts out sins as water puts out fire. So does prayer in the depth of the night. Then he took hold of his own tongue and said: hold this back.
- **Narrator:** Muʿādh ibn Jabal
- **Collection:** Musnad Ahmad, Tirmizi and Ibn Majah
- **Source:** Maʿārif al-Hadīth, vol. 1, no. 316/76, pp. 388–390 (pdf pp. 379–381)
- **Note:** The book's commentary takes the fasting and charity here to mean the voluntary kinds, beyond the obligatory pillars. That fits a card on keeping up good habits after Ramadan. "Jihad as the highest peak", which is in the full hadith, is left out to keep the card short.

### R-H30
- **Title:** Purify and pray: Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr
- **Text:** Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr and the Eid salāh were made obligatory just two days before the end of Ramadan in the second year after the Hijrah. The verse "Successful is the one who purifies himself, remembers the name of his Lord and prays" was revealed about this. ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz explained it as: successful is the one who has given his Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr and prayed the Eid salāh.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 212–213 (pdf pp. 242–243)
- **Note:** Close to S28 ("The First Ramadān", same pages), which mentions Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr in one clause. This card adds the verse and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz's explanation. Please decide whether that counts as a repeat. It is placed on day 30 (and shown on day 29 when the month may end then). It pairs with the reminder in the app: "Give your Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr before the Eid prayer. Ask your local masjid for this year's amount." **No amount is stated anywhere.** Ascent to Felicity (pp. 143–144) gives the Hanafi rules if the scholar wants a "who must pay" line.

## R-E: Eid cards (2)

### R-E01
- **Title:** The first Eid
- **Text:** After the Prophet ﷺ returned from Badr, he prayed the Eid salāh on the first of Shawwāl. It was the first Eid al-Fiṭr ever prayed. A month of fasting and a great victory, and then Eid: thank Allah for the month He let you complete.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 270 (pdf p. 300)
- **Note:** The book's passage is only two lines; the last sentence is our encouragement. The Eid-morning sunnahs (eat an odd number of dates, ghusl, scent, miswāk, best clothes, Ṣadaqat al-Fiṭr before going out) are in Ascent to Felicity, p. 114 (pdf p. 109). The scholar may want them as a short checklist on this card.

### R-E02
- **Title:** Six days of Shawwāl
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said: whoever fasts Ramadan and follows it with six days of Shawwāl, it is like fasting for a lifetime.
- **Narrator:** Abū Ayyūb
- **Collection:** Tirmidhi
- **Source:** Riyād al-Sālihīn (abridged English), no. 687, pp. 818–819 (pdf pp. 819–820)
- **Note:** Fiqh point for the scholar: the book quotes Ibn al-Mubārak that the six days may be kept together or apart. Its footnote says the same. The card says nothing about how. Not on Eid day itself (fasting on the day of Eid is not allowed; Ascent to Felicity, p. 135). Show it from 2 Shawwāl.
