# Sahabi of the Month: set A

Six Companions, 31 daily facts each, ordered as a story: lineage and early life, Islam, deeds and character, worship, death.

Sources:
- **Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah**: *The Lives of the Sahabah*, Mufti Afzal Hoosen Elias's English translation, 3 vols. Printed page = PDF page + 2 in all three volumes.
- **al-Iṣābah**: Ibn Ḥajar, *al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah* (Arabic scan, 8 vols). Printed page = PDF page − 1 in the volumes used here.

## Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq
- **Title:** The truthful one, the Prophet's ﷺ companion in the cave

### A-ABUBAKR-01
- **Text:** His name was ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUthmān, of the clan of Taym of Quraysh. His father was known as Abū Quḥāfah, and his mother was Umm al-Khayr Salmā bint Ṣakhr, his father's cousin.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, pp. 144–145 (pdf pp. 145–146)

### A-ABUBAKR-02
- **Text:** He was born two years and six months after the Year of the Elephant. ʿĀʾishah said that the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr once compared their ages, and the Prophet ﷺ was the older.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 145 (pdf p. 146)

### A-ABUBAKR-03
- **Text:** His family named him ʿAbdullāh, but the name ʿAtīq stuck. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever wished to see someone freed from the Fire should look at Abū Bakr.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 146 (pdf p. 147)

### A-ABUBAKR-04
- **Text:** He knew the family lines of the Quraysh better than anyone. He was a likeable, easy-going merchant of fine character, and people came to him for his knowledge and his good company.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 147 (pdf p. 148)

### A-ABUBAKR-05
- **Text:** He was the Prophet's ﷺ close friend even before Islam. One day he asked him why he no longer joined his people's gatherings, and why they said he spoke ill of their forefathers. The Prophet ﷺ said, "I am the Messenger of Allah, calling you to Allah," and Abū Bakr accepted Islam there and then.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 84 (pdf p. 82)

### A-ABUBAKR-06
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said that everyone he invited to Islam held back a little and had doubts, except Abū Bakr. He did not hesitate at all.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 85 (pdf p. 83)

### A-ABUBAKR-07
- **Text:** No one in Makkah was happier that day than the Prophet ﷺ. Abū Bakr went straight out and invited his friends, and ʿUthmān, Ṭalḥah, al-Zubayr and Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ all accepted Islam.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 84 (pdf p. 82)

### A-ABUBAKR-08
- **Text:** When the Muslims were only a few, he pressed the Prophet ﷺ for permission to call to Islam openly. He stood and gave the first public talk inviting people to Allah, and was beaten so badly that his face could hardly be recognised.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 291 (pdf p. 289)

### A-ABUBAKR-09
- **Text:** He did not speak until the day had ended. When he finally did, his first words were, "How is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ?" He swore he would not eat or drink until he had seen him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 291–292 (pdf pp. 289–290)

### A-ABUBAKR-10
- **Text:** Brought to the Prophet ﷺ that night, he asked him to pray for his mother, who had cared for him so well. The Prophet ﷺ made du'ā for her and invited her, and she accepted Islam.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 292 (pdf p. 290)

### A-ABUBAKR-11
- **Text:** He saw Bilāl being tortured and bought his freedom with one of his own slaves. Before the Hijrah he freed seven other slaves as well, purely for Allah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 300 (pdf p. 298)

### A-ABUBAKR-12
- **Text:** He had forty thousand when the Prophet ﷺ was sent. He spent it freeing slaves and helping the Muslims, and arrived in Madinah with only five thousand.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, pp. 147–148 (pdf pp. 148–149)

### A-ABUBAKR-13
- **Text:** When he set out to leave Makkah, a chief named Ibn al-Daghinah stopped him: "A man like you should not leave. You give to those in need, keep family ties, carry others' burdens, host guests and help in every good cause."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 294 (pdf p. 292)
- **Note:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 149 (pdf p. 150) points out that these are the same qualities Khadījah described in the Prophet ﷺ.

### A-ABUBAKR-14
- **Text:** He built a small masjid in his courtyard to pray and recite. He wept easily over the Qurʾān, and the women and children of the idol-worshippers crowded round to watch him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 294 (pdf p. 292)

### A-ABUBAKR-15
- **Text:** When told to worship only indoors or lose his protector, he gave the protection back, saying, "I am content with the protection of Allah."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 294 (pdf p. 292)

### A-ABUBAKR-16
- **Text:** After the Night Journey, people asked him if he believed such a story. He said, "If he said it, it is true. I believe him about news from the heavens morning and evening." For this he was called al-Ṣiddīq.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 104 (pdf p. 102)

### A-ABUBAKR-17
- **Text:** When the Prophet ﷺ told him that Allah had allowed the Hijrah and that he could come along, Abū Bakr wept. ʿĀʾishah said she had never seen anyone cry for joy until that day.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 341–342 (pdf pp. 339–340)

### A-ABUBAKR-18
- **Text:** He had been feeding two camels for months for this journey. He offered one to the Prophet ﷺ, who would take it only if he paid for it.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 342 (pdf p. 340)

### A-ABUBAKR-19
- **Text:** On the way to the cave he kept walking ahead, then behind. He explained that he feared an ambush in front and pursuers at the back, and he would rather any harm fell on him than on the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 343–344 (pdf pp. 341–342)

### A-ABUBAKR-20
- **Text:** He went into the cave first and cleaned out every hole before the Prophet ﷺ entered. ʿUmar later said that this one night of Abū Bakr's was better than the whole family of ʿUmar.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 344 (pdf p. 342)

### A-ABUBAKR-21
- **Text:** In the cave he whispered that the searchers would see them if they looked at their feet. The Prophet ﷺ replied, "What do you think of two when Allah is their third?"
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 344 (pdf p. 342)

### A-ABUBAKR-22
- **Text:** When a rider caught up with them on the road, Abū Bakr wept. He said, "By Allah, I am not crying for myself. I am crying for you."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 345 (pdf p. 343)

### A-ABUBAKR-23
- **Text:** He took all his money on the Hijrah. To calm her blind grandfather, Asmāʾ filled the money dish with stones and let him touch it. In truth, she said, he had left them nothing.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 195–196 (pdf pp. 193–194)

### A-ABUBAKR-24
- **Text:** ʿUmar once gave half his wealth, hoping to outdo Abū Bakr. Abū Bakr brought everything he owned. Asked what he had left his family, he said, "Allah and His Messenger." ʿUmar said he would never beat him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 182 (pdf p. 180)

### A-ABUBAKR-25
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ asked who had fasted that day, visited the sick, followed a funeral and fed a poor person. Abū Bakr had done all four, and the Prophet ﷺ said whoever does them in one day will enter Jannah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 525 (pdf p. 523)

### A-ABUBAKR-26
- **Text:** In his last illness the Prophet ﷺ said a servant had been offered this world or the Ākhirah, and had chosen the Ākhirah. Only Abū Bakr understood. He wept and said, "May our fathers, mothers, selves and wealth be given for you."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 353–354 (pdf pp. 351–352)

### A-ABUBAKR-27
- **Text:** Before he became caliph he used to milk the neighbours' goats. A little girl feared that would stop. He promised it would not, and kept milking for them, asking if they wanted it frothy or not.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 168 (pdf p. 166)

### A-ABUBAKR-28
- **Text:** Once he was handed honey mixed with water and burst into tears. He recalled seeing the Prophet ﷺ push the world away from himself, and feared the world might take hold of him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 299 (pdf p. 297)

### A-ABUBAKR-29
- **Text:** Seeing a bird on a tree, he said, "How lucky you are. You eat fruit and fly away, with no reckoning ahead of you. I wish I were like you."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 147 (pdf p. 145)

### A-ABUBAKR-30
- **Text:** Nothing could distract him in salāh. Mujāhid said that Ibn al-Zubayr stood in prayer as still as a stick, just as Abū Bakr used to.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 178 (pdf p. 176)

### A-ABUBAKR-31
- **Text:** Before he died he asked to be shrouded in his old clothes, saying the living need new clothes more than the dead. He passed away on a Monday in Jumādā al-Ūlā, 13 AH, aged sixty-three.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 75 (pdf p. 73); al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 150 (pdf p. 151)

## ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb
- **Title:** al-Fārūq, the second caliph

### A-UMAR-01
- **Text:** ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb ibn Nufayl was from the clan of ʿAdī of Quraysh. His kunya was Abū Ḥafṣ, and he is known as Amīr al-Muʾminīn. His mother was Ḥantamah bint Hāshim of the Makhzūm clan.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 484 (pdf p. 485)

### A-UMAR-02
- **Text:** He was born about thirty years before the Prophet ﷺ was sent with the message.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 484 (pdf p. 485)
- **Note:** This is the report from Ibn Isḥāq (four years after the great Fijār war). Al-Iṣābah also mentions thirteen years after the Year of the Elephant.

### A-UMAR-03
- **Text:** Before Islam the Quraysh chose him as their envoy to speak for them with other tribes.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 484 (pdf p. 485)

### A-UMAR-04
- **Text:** At first he was harsh towards the Muslims. The Prophet ﷺ prayed, "O Allah, strengthen Islam through whichever of these two men You love more: Abū Jahl or ʿUmar." ʿUmar was the one Allah loved more.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, pp. 484–485 (pdf pp. 485–486)

### A-UMAR-05
- **Text:** He once went out to confront the Prophet ﷺ, found him already in the masjid, and stood behind him as he recited Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah. ʿUmar thought, "A poet," and the next verse said it was no poet's word. He thought, "A soothsayer," and the verse after answered that too. Islam settled into his heart.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 486 (pdf p. 487)

### A-UMAR-06
- **Text:** He came to the Prophet ﷺ in a house near Ṣafā. The Prophet ﷺ took hold of his collar, said, "Son of al-Khaṭṭāb, accept Islam," and prayed for his guidance. ʿUmar bore witness, and the Muslims cried "Allāhu Akbar" so loudly that it was heard in the streets of Makkah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 86 (pdf p. 84)

### A-UMAR-07
- **Text:** Ibn Masʿūd said that the Muslims could not worship Allah openly until ʿUmar accepted Islam. His Islam was a victory for them and a release from hardship.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 484 (pdf p. 485)

### A-UMAR-08
- **Text:** He asked why they should hide the truth. He went round the Kaʿbah and to every gathering he used to sit in, declaring his faith. Then the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims prayed Ẓuhr openly at the Kaʿbah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 292–293 (pdf pp. 290–291)

### A-UMAR-09
- **Text:** He then walked out with the Prophet ﷺ, Ḥamzah and the Companions who had been hiding in the house of al-Arqam, and the Quraysh saw that the Muslims now had protection. That day the Prophet ﷺ named him al-Fārūq.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 486 (pdf p. 487)

### A-UMAR-10
- **Text:** On the Hijrah, his friend ʿAyyāsh was being lured back to Makkah. ʿUmar offered him half of his own wealth if he would stay, and when he still went, gave him his best camel to escape on.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 348–349 (pdf pp. 346–347)

### A-UMAR-11
- **Text:** Some feared ʿUmar would be too stern as caliph. Abū Bakr, on his deathbed, said he would tell Allah, "I have placed over them the best of Your creation."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 69 (pdf p. 68)
- **Note:** Vol. 2 has printed page = PDF page + 1 before about p. 136.

### A-UMAR-12
- **Text:** In the year of famine he fed the crowds who came to Madinah. At its height ten thousand ate at his table each night, and fifty thousand more were fed where they stayed. He ate nothing from his own family's homes, only the food cooked for the hungry, until the rain came.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 228–229 (pdf pp. 226–227)

### A-UMAR-13
- **Text:** In that famine he gave up meat and ghee and lived on oil until the colour of his skin changed.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 484 (pdf p. 485)

### A-UMAR-14
- **Text:** On his night rounds he found a mother boiling only water to quiet her hungry children. He wept, filled a sack from the treasury and carried it on his own back, saying, "I will be questioned about them in the Ākhirah."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 229 (pdf p. 227)

### A-UMAR-15
- **Text:** He cooked for that family himself, blowing on the fire, and served them. Then he sat nearby and would not leave until he saw the children laughing and playing.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 229–230 (pdf pp. 227–228)

### A-UMAR-16
- **Text:** When he fell ill and honey was prescribed, he would not take the honey in the treasury until he had asked the people from the minbar and they agreed.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 265 (pdf p. 263)

### A-UMAR-17
- **Text:** He refused a loan from the treasury for his trade. If he died before repaying, he said, people might let it go, and he would answer for it on the Day of Judgement.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 265 (pdf p. 263)

### A-UMAR-18
- **Text:** His daughter Ḥafṣah asked for a share of some wealth for the family. He told her his relatives had a right only to his own money. This belonged to the Muslims.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 265 (pdf p. 263)

### A-UMAR-19
- **Text:** When the treasures of Persia were laid before him, he wept. On a day of victory he feared that wealth brings enmity and hatred among people.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 287 (pdf p. 285)

### A-UMAR-20
- **Text:** Though he ruled from Persia to Shām, delegations found him wearing the same robe with twelve patches.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 302 (pdf p. 300)

### A-UMAR-21
- **Text:** Asked to take a bigger allowance, he said: the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr were like two travellers who reached their goal. If the third keeps to their road and their provisions, he will join them.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 301 (pdf p. 299)

### A-UMAR-22
- **Text:** As caliph he told the people from the minbar that he once grazed his aunts' animals for a handful of dates and raisins. He said he had felt some pride and wanted to remind himself who he really was.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 574 (pdf p. 572)

### A-UMAR-23
- **Text:** On a hot day he asked a boy for a ride on his donkey. The boy jumped off for him, but ʿUmar insisted the boy stay in front and rode behind him into Madinah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 574 (pdf p. 572)

### A-UMAR-24
- **Text:** An old woman, Khawlah, stopped him on the road and spoke to him sharply. He stood and listened, saying, "Why should I not listen to her when Allah listened to her?" and recalled the verse revealed about her.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 453–454 (pdf pp. 451–452)

### A-UMAR-25
- **Text:** He once picked up a twig and said, "I wish I had been this twig. I wish I had been nothing at all."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 148 (pdf p. 146)

### A-UMAR-26
- **Text:** He said that if all people were to enter Jannah but one, he would fear it was him. And if all were to enter the Fire but one, he would hope it was him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 148 (pdf p. 146)

### A-UMAR-27
- **Text:** Leading Fajr, he wept aloud while reciting Sūrah Yūsuf at "I complain of my grief and sorrow only to Allah." His crying could be heard three rows back.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 178 (pdf p. 176)

### A-UMAR-28
- **Text:** He prayed long at night. At midnight he woke his family with "Salāh!" and recited the verse telling believers to command their families to pray and be steadfast in it.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 186 (pdf p. 184)

### A-UMAR-29
- **Text:** After he was stabbed he lay unconscious. Someone called out, "Salāh, O Amīr al-Muʾminīn!" He woke at once and prayed while his wound bled, saying there is no share in Islam for one who abandons salāh.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 136 (pdf p. 134)

### A-UMAR-30
- **Text:** As he lay dying he wept, not knowing where he would end up. ʿAlī comforted him: he had often heard the Prophet ﷺ say that Abū Bakr and ʿUmar would be the leaders of the mature people of Jannah.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 92 (pdf p. 90)

### A-UMAR-31
- **Text:** He asked ʿĀʾishah to let him be buried beside his two companions. She had kept the place for herself but gave it to him, and he was laid to rest beside the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 72 (pdf p. 71)

## ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān
- **Title:** Dhū al-Nūrayn, the third caliph

### A-UTHMAN-01
- **Text:** ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān ibn Abī al-ʿĀṣ was from the Umayyah clan of Quraysh. His kunyas were Abū ʿAbdillāh and Abū ʿAmr, and he is known as Amīr al-Muʾminīn.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 377 (pdf p. 378)

### A-UTHMAN-02
- **Text:** His mother was Arwā bint Kurayz, who accepted Islam. Her mother, al-Bayḍāʾ bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, was the Prophet's ﷺ aunt.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 377 (pdf p. 378)

### A-UTHMAN-03
- **Text:** He was born six years after the Year of the Elephant. He was of medium height, with a handsome face, fine skin, a full beard and broad shoulders.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 377 (pdf p. 378)

### A-UTHMAN-04
- **Text:** He was at his aunt's house when the Prophet ﷺ came in. The Prophet ﷺ said "Lā ilāha illallāh" and recited verses from Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt. ʿUthmān followed him out and accepted Islam.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, pp. 86–87 (pdf pp. 84–85)

### A-UTHMAN-05
- **Text:** When his uncle found out, he tied him up with rope and swore not to free him until he left Islam. ʿUthmān swore he never would. Seeing how firm he was, his uncle let him go.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 297 (pdf p. 295)

### A-UTHMAN-06
- **Text:** He married the Prophet's ﷺ daughter Ruqayyah. After she passed away he married her sister Umm Kulthūm, and so he was called Dhū al-Nūrayn, "the one with two lights".
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 377 (pdf p. 378)

### A-UTHMAN-07
- **Text:** ʿAlī said of him, "That is a man who is called Dhū al-Nūrayn in the highest assembly above."
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, pp. 377–378 (pdf pp. 378–379)

### A-UTHMAN-08
- **Text:** He and Ruqayyah were the first family to make the Hijrah for Allah's sake, to Abyssinia. The Prophet ﷺ prayed for them and said ʿUthmān was the first since Lūṭ to emigrate with his family.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 350 (pdf p. 348)

### A-UTHMAN-09
- **Text:** He stayed behind from Badr to nurse Ruqayyah in her illness. The Prophet ﷺ still gave him his share and his reward, as if he had been there.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 4, p. 378 (pdf p. 379)

### A-UTHMAN-10
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ told his daughter to look after ʿUthmān well, because of all his Companions, ʿUthmān's character was closest to his own.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 554 (pdf p. 552)

### A-UTHMAN-11
- **Text:** The Muslims in Madinah had to pay for water. The Prophet ﷺ asked who would buy the well of Rūmah and give it to them. ʿUthmān bought it for thirty-five thousand dirhams and gave it as charity for all.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 209–210 (pdf pp. 207–208)

### A-UTHMAN-12
- **Text:** For the expedition of Tabūk the Prophet ﷺ urged the Companions three times to give. Each time ʿUthmān stood and pledged another hundred camels with their saddles.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 196 (pdf p. 194)

### A-UTHMAN-13
- **Text:** He also poured a thousand dinars into the Prophet's ﷺ lap. Turning the coins over in his hands, the Prophet ﷺ said again and again that nothing ʿUthmān did after that day would harm him.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 197 (pdf p. 195)

### A-UTHMAN-14
- **Text:** ʿUthmān said that one dirham given by someone who is short of money is better than ten thousand given by someone who has plenty left.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 182 (pdf p. 180)

### A-UTHMAN-15
- **Text:** Once the Prophet ﷺ saw him leading a camel loaded with flour, butter and honey. The Prophet ﷺ had it cooked in a pot and shared it with the Companions.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 213 (pdf p. 211)

### A-UTHMAN-16
- **Text:** At Ḥudaybiyah the Prophet ﷺ sent him to Makkah. The Muslims thought he was lucky to be able to do ṭawāf. The Prophet ﷺ said he would not, even if he stayed for years, until the Prophet ﷺ had done it first.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 386–387 (pdf pp. 384–385)

### A-UTHMAN-17
- **Text:** In Makkah his cousin urged him to do ṭawāf. He replied, "We do nothing until our guide does it, and then we follow in his footsteps."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 387 (pdf p. 385)

### A-BILAL-14
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ made him a brother in Islam to Abū ʿUbaydah ibn al-Jarrāḥ.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 1, p. 455 (pdf p. 456)

### A-BILAL-15
- **Text:** He was present with the Prophet ﷺ at every battle.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 1, p. 455 (pdf p. 456)

### A-BILAL-16
- **Text:** He looked after the Prophet's ﷺ household money. When a poor Muslim came, the Prophet ﷺ would tell him to borrow to buy clothes or food for him.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 1, p. 455 (pdf p. 456); Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 236 (pdf p. 234)

### A-BILAL-17
- **Text:** Once a lender threatened to take him as a slave if the debt was not paid within days. Bilāl spent a sleepless night. At dawn he was called: four camels laden with goods had arrived, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "Rejoice, Allah has sent what will pay your debt."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 236–237 (pdf pp. 234–235)

### A-BILAL-18
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ found him keeping heaps of dates for future guests. He told him, "Spend, Bilāl, and do not fear that the Lord of the Throne will leave you short."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 178 (pdf p. 176)

### A-BILAL-19
- **Text:** At Tabūk, when hungry Companions came late, Bilāl shook out his bag and found only seven dates. The Prophet ﷺ said Bismillāh over them, all ate their fill, and the seven dates were still there.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 628 (pdf p. 626)

### A-BILAL-20
- **Text:** He came one dawn to call the Prophet ﷺ and found him weeping. Bilāl asked why, since Allah had forgiven him. The Prophet ﷺ replied, "Should I not be a grateful servant?"
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 177 (pdf p. 175)

### A-BILAL-21
- **Text:** One morning the Prophet ﷺ came out late for Fajr. He told Bilāl he had been praying the two sunnah rakʿahs, and that even if it had grown lighter still, he would have prayed them well.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 180 (pdf p. 178)

### A-BILAL-22
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ said, "Bilāl, I heard your footsteps ahead of me in Jannah." Bilāl explained that whenever he sinned he prayed two rakʿahs, and whenever his wuḍūʾ broke he renewed it at once and prayed two rakʿahs.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 194 (pdf p. 192)

### A-BILAL-23
- **Text:** On an icy morning he called the Fajr adhān twice and no one came because of the cold. The Prophet ﷺ prayed for Allah to take the cold away, and the people came to pray with ease.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 3, p. 647 (pdf p. 645)

### A-BILAL-24
- **Text:** Abū Bakr once worried he had upset Bilāl, Salmān and Ṣuhayb. He went back and asked, "Brothers, have I made you angry?" They replied, "No. May Allah forgive you, brother."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 428 (pdf p. 426)

### A-BILAL-25
- **Text:** He advised ʿUmar's servant Aslam: if ʿUmar ever grows angry, recite the Qurʾān until his anger passes.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 556 (pdf p. 554)

### A-BILAL-26
- **Text:** When he proposed marriage to a family, he said, "We were slaves and Allah freed us. We were astray and Allah guided us. If you accept, praise be to Allah. If you refuse, Allah is still the Greatest."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 686 (pdf p. 684)

### A-BILAL-27
- **Text:** A family would agree to a marriage for his brother only if Bilāl came. He came, and honestly told them his brother was weak in character and practice. They said, "We will marry anyone who is your brother."
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, p. 686 (pdf p. 684)

### A-BILAL-28
- **Text:** After the Prophet ﷺ passed away, Bilāl called the adhān before his burial, and the people in the masjid wept at "I bear witness that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah." After that he did not wish to call the adhān for anyone else.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 447 (pdf p. 445)

### A-BILAL-29
- **Text:** He asked Abū Bakr, "Did you free me for yourself or for Allah?" "For Allah," he said. "Then let me go and strive in Allah's path." He left for Shām.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 1, p. 447 (pdf p. 445)

### A-BILAL-30
- **Text:** When ʿUmar visited Shām, Bilāl told him plainly that the commanders ate well while their soldiers did not. ʿUmar agreed and made each commander promise proper rations for his men.
- **Source:** Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, vol. 2, pp. 235–236 (pdf pp. 233–234)

### A-BILAL-31
- **Text:** He spent the rest of his life in Shām and died there during the caliphate of ʿUmar.
- **Source:** al-Iṣābah, vol. 1, p. 456 (pdf p. 457)
- **Note:** Al-Iṣābah quotes Bukhari for this. It also mentions the plague of ʿAmwās and the year 20 AH, and differing reports on where he was buried.
