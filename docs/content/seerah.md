# Weekly Sīrah cards (S01–S52)

Source for every card: *Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged Version)*, Moulana Muhammad Idrees Kaandhlawi, tr. Mufti Muhammad Kadwa and Moulana Mahommed Mahommedy, Jamiatul Ulama (KZN) Ta'limi Board, 2nd ed. 2015. One volume. Printed page = PDF page − 30.

Order: S01–S17 Makkah (to the eve of the Hijrah), S18–S45 Madinah, S46–S52 final year and passing.

### S01
- **Title:** A Messenger from the Best
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ recited, "A messenger has come to you from among yourselves," and explained that he came from the most noble of their families, and that from Ādam down to him every ancestor was born within marriage. Even Abū Sufyān, before Islam, told the Roman emperor that he was a man of high lineage. Begin this year of his sīrah by thanking Allah for sending a Messenger from the best of people.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 1 (pdf p. 31)

### S02
- **Title:** A Name Meaning "the Praised"
- **Text:** On the seventh day after his birth, his grandfather ʿAbd al-Muttalib performed the ʿaqīqah and named him Muhammad. When the Quraysh asked why he chose a name no one in the family had carried, he said he wished him to be praised by Allah and by His creation on earth. Each time we send salawāt upon him ﷺ, we join in that praise.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 19–21 (pdf pp. 49–51)

### S03
- **Title:** Halīmah Chooses the Orphan
- **Text:** The women of Banū Saʿd came to Makkah for infants to nurse, and each passed over the orphan Muhammad ﷺ, since no father would reward them. Halīmah would not return empty-handed, so she took him, hoping for barakah from Allah. Her milk flowed, her weak camel raced ahead, and in a year of drought her goats came home full. Do good hoping in Allah, not in people's reward, and make room for the orphan.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 22–24 (pdf pp. 52–54)

### S04
- **Title:** Al-Amīn Keeps His Word
- **Text:** Before prophethood the people of Makkah called him al-Amīn, the trustworthy. A man who owed him money promised to return shortly, then forgot for three days; when he came back he found the Prophet ﷺ still waiting there, saying only that he had been put to difficulty. His trading partners said he never delayed or quarrelled. Keep your promises and be easy to deal with.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 30–31 (pdf pp. 60–61)

### S05
- **Title:** Marriage to Khadījah
- **Text:** Khadījah, a noble and wealthy woman, sent the Prophet ﷺ to Syria with her goods because of his honesty, and she gained more profit than ever before. She then proposed marriage. In the nikāh sermon Abū Tālib said that he lacked wealth, but wealth is a passing shadow, and in nobility and intellect he outweighed any young man of Quraysh. In marriage, look first to character, not wealth.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 31–33 (pdf pp. 61–63)

### S06
- **Title:** The Black Stone and Fairness
- **Text:** When the Quraysh rebuilt the Kaʿbah, the clans nearly fought over who would set the Black Stone in place. They agreed to accept the first man to enter the Haram next morning, and it was the Prophet ﷺ; they cried out that the trustworthy one had come. He laid the stone on a sheet, had each chief hold an edge, then set it with his own hands. A fair solution that lets everyone share in the good can end a quarrel.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 34–36 (pdf pp. 64–66)

### S07
- **Title:** "Read!" in the Cave of Hirāʾ
- **Text:** At forty, in the cave of Hirāʾ, the angel came and told the Prophet ﷺ to read, and the first verses of Sūrat al-ʿAlaq were revealed. He came home trembling, and Khadījah comforted him: Allah would never disgrace him, for he kept family ties, spoke the truth, carried others' burdens, helped the poor and honoured guests. Such character draws Allah's protection; build it in ordinary days.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 41–42 (pdf pp. 71–72)

### S08
- **Title:** The First Lesson: Wudūʾ and Salāh
- **Text:** After belief in Allah and His Messenger, the very first lesson the Prophet ﷺ received was wudūʾ and salāh. Jibrīl struck the ground so that a spring flowed, made wudūʾ as the Prophet ﷺ watched, then prayed two rakʿahs with him; the Prophet ﷺ went home and taught Khadījah the same. Salāh came first after faith, so let it come first in our day too.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 44–45 (pdf pp. 74–75)

### S09
- **Title:** Abū Bakr Answers Without Hesitation
- **Text:** When the Prophet ﷺ invited his close friend Abū Bakr to Islam, he accepted at once; the Prophet ﷺ said that everyone else he invited hesitated a little, but not Abū Bakr. Abū Bakr then invited his own friends, and ʿUthmān, al-Zubayr, ʿAbd al-Rahmān ibn ʿAwf, Talhah and Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqās accepted Islam through him. Answer the truth without delay, and share it with those you love.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 48 (pdf p. 78)

### S10
- **Title:** The Call from Mount Safā
- **Text:** After three years of quiet invitation, Allah commanded the Prophet ﷺ to call openly and warn his nearest relatives. He climbed Safā, called each clan by name and asked if they would believe him were he to warn of an army behind the hill. They said they had only known him to be truthful, and he warned them of a severe punishment. A life of honesty gives weight to the truth we speak.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 58 (pdf p. 88)

### S11
- **Title:** The Sun and the Moon
- **Text:** When the Quraysh pressed Abū Tālib to stop his nephew, he asked the Prophet ﷺ not to load him beyond what he could bear. With tears in his eyes the Prophet ﷺ said that even if they put the sun in his right hand and the moon in his left, he would not leave this work until Allah made the dīn prevail or he died. Abū Tālib then promised never to hand him over. Hold firm to the dīn when others press you to let go.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 60 (pdf p. 90)

### S12
- **Title:** Bilāl: "Ahad, Ahad"
- **Text:** Bilāl ibn Rabāh was a slave of Umayyah ibn Khalaf, who laid him on the burning stones at midday with a rock on his chest and told him to deny the Prophet ﷺ. Through all of it Bilāl said only, "Ahad, Ahad": He is One. Abū Bakr bought him and set him free, and the scars stayed on his back for life. Hold on to the oneness of Allah when it is tested.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 89 (pdf p. 119)

### S13
- **Title:** Jaʿfar Before the Negus
- **Text:** In the court of the Negus, Jaʿfar ibn Abī Tālib explained that his people had worshipped idols, cut family ties and oppressed the weak, until a truthful Messenger taught them to worship Allah alone, speak the truth and treat neighbours well. When he recited the opening of Sūrat Maryam, the king wept until his beard was wet, and refused to hand the Muslims over. Let our conduct explain Islam as clearly as our words.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 102–103 (pdf pp. 132–133)

### S14
- **Title:** The Prayer that Brought ʿUmar
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ used to pray that Allah would strengthen Islam through ʿUmar ibn al-Khattāb, and the book calls this duʿāʾ the real cause of his Islam. ʿUmar set out with his sword, read verses of Sūrat Tā Hā at his sister's house, then went to the Prophet ﷺ and declared his faith, and the Prophet ﷺ called out the takbīr for joy. Keep praying for the guidance of others, even those who oppose the truth.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 107–110 (pdf pp. 137–140)

### S15
- **Title:** The Duʿāʾ of Tāʾif
- **Text:** After losing Abū Tālib and Khadījah, the Prophet ﷺ went to Tāʾif, where its leaders mocked him and set youths to stone him until his feet bled. Resting in a garden, he complained only to Allah, saying that as long as Allah was not angry with him, he did not mind. Offered the destruction of the people, he refused, hoping their children would worship Allah. Take your pain to Allah, and hope for good even for those who hurt you.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 116–120 (pdf pp. 146–150)

### S16
- **Title:** The Gift of Five Salāhs
- **Text:** On the night of Miʿrāj, after the trials of Tāʾif, Allah honoured the Prophet ﷺ with gifts for his Ummah, among them the daily salāh. Fifty prayers were first prescribed; at Mūsā's urging the Prophet ﷺ asked again and again for less until five remained, and it was declared that these five carry the reward of fifty. Each of our five salāhs is a gift from that night, so guard them all.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 125, 136–138 (pdf pp. 155, 166–168)

### S17
- **Title:** Musʿab: "Sit and Listen"
- **Text:** After the first pledge at ʿAqabah, the Prophet ﷺ sent Musʿab ibn ʿUmayr to Madinah to teach the Qurʾān. When Usayd ibn Hudayr and then Saʿd ibn Muʿādh came to him angry, sword in hand, Musʿab only asked them to sit and listen, and leave if they disliked it. Both heard the Qurʾān and accepted Islam, and Saʿd's whole clan followed by nightfall. Invite with calm; a gentle word opens hearts that anger closes.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 147–148 (pdf pp. 177–178)

### S18
- **Title:** Returning Trusts on the Hijrah Night
- **Text:** On the night the Quraysh surrounded his house to kill him, the Prophet ﷺ had ʿAlī lie in his bed. Even his enemies still left their valuables with him, trusting his honesty, so he told ʿAlī to return each item to its owner. ʿAlī stayed in Makkah three days to do this before joining him at Qubāʾ. Return what is entrusted to you, even to those who treat you badly.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 162, 171 (pdf pp. 192, 201)

### S19
- **Title:** "Allah Is with Us"
- **Text:** During the Hijrah, the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr hid for three nights in the cave of Thawr while the Quraysh searched for them. When the searchers reached the mouth of the cave, Abū Bakr feared they would be seen. The Prophet ﷺ asked what he thought of two when Allah was their third, and said, "Do not grieve; Allah is with us." In moments of fear, remember that Allah is with those who trust Him.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 164–165 (pdf pp. 194–195)
- **Note:** The book spells the cave "Saur".

### S20
- **Title:** Umm Maʿbad's Goat
- **Text:** On the road to Madinah the Prophet ﷺ stopped at the tent of Umm Maʿbad, who had no food to sell. With her permission he said Bismillāh and milked a weak goat that had no milk, and it filled a large vessel. He gave her to drink first, then his companions, and drank last himself, then filled the vessel again and left it for her. Serve others first and take your own share last.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 167–168 (pdf pp. 197–198)

### S21
- **Title:** The Masjid at Qubāʾ
- **Text:** The first thing the Prophet ﷺ did on reaching Qubāʾ was to lay the foundation of a masjid, placing the first stone himself and carrying heavy stones with his Companions. He later visited it every Saturday to pray two rakʿahs, and said that whoever makes wudūʾ at home and prays two rakʿahs in Masjid Qubāʾ gains the reward of an ʿumrah. Make the masjid the centre of your life.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 171–172 (pdf pp. 201–202)

### S22
- **Title:** The First Jumuʿah Khutbah
- **Text:** On the way from Qubāʾ into Madinah, the time of Jumuʿah came among Banū Sālim, and there the Prophet ﷺ led the first Jumuʿah. In his khutbah he urged taqwā, calling it the best advice one Muslim can give another, and said that whoever puts right his outward and inward dealings with Allah, seeking only His pleasure, gains honour here and treasure after death. Let each Friday renew your taqwā.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 173–175 (pdf pp. 203–205)

### S23
- **Title:** Four Deeds to Jannah
- **Text:** ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām, a learned rabbi of Madinah, went to see the Prophet ﷺ when he arrived and knew at once that this was not the face of a liar. The first words he heard from him were: feed people, spread the salām, keep family ties and pray at night while people sleep, and you will enter Jannah with ease. All four are within anyone's reach; strengthen one of them this week.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 182–183 (pdf pp. 212–213)

### S24
- **Title:** Building the Prophet's Masjid
- **Text:** The land for the Prophet's Masjid belonged to two orphans who offered it as a gift, but the Prophet ﷺ would only take it at a price. He made and carried bricks with the Muhājirūn and Ansār, saying: O Allah, there is no good except the good of the Hereafter, so help the Ansār and the Muhājirūn. Its pillars were palm trunks and its roof palm leaves. Deal fairly with the weak, and work for the Hereafter.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 189–190 (pdf pp. 219–220)

### S25
- **Title:** Brotherhood and Duʿāʾ
- **Text:** In Madinah the Prophet ﷺ joined each Muhājir to one of the Ansār as a brother, and the Ansār shared so generously that the Muhājirūn feared the Ansār would take all the reward. The Prophet ﷺ told them it would not be so, as long as they kept making duʿāʾ for them. When someone does you good and you cannot repay them, pray for them sincerely.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 195–198 (pdf pp. 225–228)

### S26
- **Title:** How the Adhān Began
- **Text:** The Muslims needed a call to gather for salāh, and the Prophet ﷺ disliked the bell, horn and fire used by others. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Zayd saw in a dream a man who taught him the words of the adhān and iqāmah; the Prophet ﷺ called it a true dream and had him teach Bilāl, whose voice was louder. ʿUmar then came saying he had seen the same. Answer the adhān gladly; it began as a true dream given to the Companions.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 199–201 (pdf pp. 229–231)

### S27
- **Title:** One Bowl for the Suffah
- **Text:** The Ahl al-Suffah were poor Companions who stayed by the masjid to learn the Qurʾān. Once, when Abū Hurayrah sat by the road weak with hunger, the Prophet ﷺ read his face, smiled and took him home. Finding a bowl of milk, he had him call all the people of the Suffah; each drank their fill, then Abū Hurayrah, and the Prophet ﷺ drank last. Notice the needy, and share what you have.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 207–209 (pdf pp. 237–239)

### S28
- **Title:** The First Ramadān
- **Text:** In the second year after the Hijrah, in the last days of Shaʿbān, fasting the month of Ramadān was made obligatory. Before that month ended, Sadaqat al-Fitr and the Eid salāh were enjoined, and in the same year the Eid al-Adhā salāh and qurbānī. Every fast, Eid and charity of ours goes back to that year; keep them with the same care.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 212–213 (pdf pp. 242–243)

### S29
- **Title:** The Duʿāʾ at Badr
- **Text:** At Badr the Muslims were just over three hundred and poorly equipped, facing about a thousand. After setting the ranks, the Prophet ﷺ prayed two rakʿahs, then pleaded with Allah, now in sajdah, now with hands raised, until his cloak slipped from his shoulders, asking Him to fulfil His promise so that He would still be worshipped on earth. Do what you can, then turn to Allah with humble, persistent duʿāʾ.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 240–241 (pdf pp. 270–271)

### S30
- **Title:** A Simple Nikāh
- **Text:** When ʿAlī wished to marry Fātimah, he had nothing to give, so the Prophet ﷺ had him offer his armour from Badr as her mahr. ʿAlī sold it for 480 dirhams, and the Prophet ﷺ told him to buy perfume and clothing with it. Her household goods were a quilt, a leather mattress stuffed with bark, two hand-mills, a water-skin and two clay pots. The best of marriages began simply; keep nikāh easy.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 281–282 (pdf pp. 311–312)

### S31
- **Title:** "Forgive My People"
- **Text:** At Uhud the Prophet ﷺ was wounded in the face. Wiping away the blood, he asked how a people could succeed who bloodied their Prophet while he called them to their Lord, yet Ibn Masʿūd remembered him praying: O my Lord, forgive my people, for they do not know. Some Quraysh leaders he had spoken against at Uhud later accepted Islam. Pray for those who hurt you; Allah may yet guide them.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 312 (pdf p. 342)

### S32
- **Title:** Anas ibn al-Nadr's Promise
- **Text:** Anas ibn al-Nadr grieved that he had missed Badr and told the Prophet ﷺ that if Allah let him take part again, Allah would see what he would do. At Uhud he went forward saying he could smell the fragrance of Jannah beyond the mountain, and was martyred with more than eighty wounds. The verse about men true to their covenant with Allah was revealed about him. Make sincere intentions, then keep them.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 314 (pdf p. 344)

### S33
- **Title:** The Short Shroud of Musʿab
- **Text:** Musʿab ibn ʿUmayr carried the flag at Uhud and was martyred defending the Prophet ﷺ. His shroud was so short that covering his head bared his feet, so his head was covered and his feet laid with izkhir grass. As the martyrs were buried two or three to a grave, the Prophet ﷺ placed first whoever knew the most Qurʾān. Honour with Allah lies in the Qurʾān we carry, not in what we wear.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 306, 327 (pdf pp. 336, 357)

### S34
- **Title:** "Who Will Protect You?"
- **Text:** Returning from an expedition in 4 AH, the Prophet ﷺ rested under a tree where he had hung his sword. A man took it and asked who would protect him now; he calmly answered, "Allah," and the sword fell from the man's hand. The Prophet ﷺ forgave him, and he later accepted Islam and brought many of his people. Rely on Allah in danger, and forgive when you have the upper hand.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 347 (pdf p. 377)

### S35
- **Title:** Digging the Trench
- **Text:** When ten thousand fighters marched on Madinah, Salmān al-Fārisī suggested a trench, and the Prophet ﷺ dug beside his Companions in the winter cold until his stomach was covered in dust. They had not eaten for days, and when they struck a huge rock he came down himself, a stone tied to his stomach from hunger, and broke it. Share the hardship of those around you, as he did.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 365–367 (pdf pp. 395–397)

### S36
- **Title:** Thumāmah Is Won by Kindness
- **Text:** Thumāmah ibn Uthāl, a chief of Banū Hanīfah, was brought captive and tied to a pillar of the masjid, where he could watch the Muslims pray. Three days running the Prophet ﷺ asked him what he thought, then set him free without asking anything. Thumāmah bathed, returned and declared his faith, saying the face, religion and city he had hated most were now dearest to him. Generosity can win a heart that force never could.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 381–382 (pdf pp. 411–412)
- **Note:** The book spells him "Sumaamah bin Usaal".

### S37
- **Title:** The Pledge Under the Tree
- **Text:** At Hudaybiyyah the Prophet ﷺ sent ʿUthmān into Makkah to say the Muslims had come only for ʿumrah. The Quraysh let him alone perform tawāf, but he refused to do it without the Prophet ﷺ. When a false rumour of his death spread, the Companions pledged under a tree to stand firm, the Prophet ﷺ pledged with his own hand for ʿUthmān, and Allah declared He was pleased with them. Put the Prophet ﷺ before your own wishes.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 396–398 (pdf pp. 426–428)

### S38
- **Title:** Hudaybiyyah: A Clear Victory
- **Text:** The Companions found the terms of the treaty of Hudaybiyyah hard to bear, yet on the way home Sūrat al-Fath was revealed, calling it a clear victory. With peace, people mixed freely, heard the Qurʾān and saw the Muslims' character, and more accepted Islam before the conquest of Makkah than in all the years before. What looks like a setback may be an opening from Allah; trust His plan.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 405 (pdf p. 435)

### S39
- **Title:** Justice at Khaybar
- **Text:** After Khaybar, its Jewish farmers kept working the land and gave half the harvest. The Prophet ﷺ sent ʿAbd Allāh ibn Rawāhah to assess the crops; he would split the harvest into two equal parts and let them take whichever they preferred. They said it was by such justice that the heavens and earth stand, and he told them his dislike of them would never make him unjust. Be fair even to those you dislike.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 446 (pdf p. 476)

### S40
- **Title:** The Fajr that Was Missed
- **Text:** Returning from Khaybar, the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions stopped to rest late at night, and none of them woke until the sun was bright. He woke first and had them leave that valley; at the next stop Bilāl gave the adhān, they made wudūʾ, prayed the two sunnah rakʿahs of Fajr and then the missed Fajr in congregation. If you miss a salāh, do not despair; make it up without delay.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 449 (pdf p. 479)

### S41
- **Title:** Entering Makkah with Bowed Head
- **Text:** In Ramadān of 8 AH the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah, the city that had once driven him out, as a conqueror. He did not ride in like a king: his head was bowed so low in humility that his beard touched the saddle, and he was reciting Sūrat al-Fath in a beautiful voice, seeing the victory as Allah's favour alone. Meet every success with humility and gratitude, not pride.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 482 (pdf p. 512)

### S42
- **Title:** "No Blame on You Today"
- **Text:** At the door of the Kaʿbah the Prophet ﷺ declared that pride in ancestry was ended, for all people are from Ādam and Ādam was made from soil. He asked the Quraysh what they expected; they hoped for kindness, and he answered as Yūsuf did his brothers: no blame on you today, go, you are free. He then returned the Kaʿbah's key to ʿUthmān ibn Talhah. Forgive when you have the power to take revenge.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 485–486 (pdf pp. 515–516)

### S43
- **Title:** His Foster-Sister After Hunayn
- **Text:** After Hunayn, a captive woman said she was the Prophet's foster-sister and showed a mark where he had bitten her as a child. He recognised it, spread his shawl for her and his eyes filled with tears; she accepted Islam and went home with gifts. When Hawāzin, the tribe of his nurse, pleaded for their families, he gave up his own share first, and six thousand captives were freed. Honour old bonds of kindness.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 510–511 (pdf pp. 540–541)
- **Note:** The book names her "Shima'" (usually known as Shaymāʾ).

### S44
- **Title:** Giving for Tabūk
- **Text:** For the hard march to Tabūk, Abū Bakr brought all his wealth, saying he had left Allah and His Messenger for his family; ʿUmar brought half, and ʿUthmān gave three hundred laden camels and a thousand dinars. Some poor Companions found no mount to ride and turned away weeping that they had nothing to spend, and Allah mentioned them in the Qurʾān. Give what you can; when you have little, let your sincere longing count.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 521–522 (pdf pp. 551–552)

### S45
- **Title:** Dimām Holds to the Basics
- **Text:** Dimām ibn Thaʿlabah came for his tribe and asked the Prophet ﷺ, under oath, if Allah had sent him and commanded five daily salāhs, a month of fasting and zakāh from the rich for the poor; to each he said yes. Dimām believed and swore to add nothing and leave nothing out, and the Prophet ﷺ said he would enter Jannah if true to his word. Hold firmly to the basic duties; they are the road to Jannah.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 546 (pdf p. 576)
- **Note:** The book spells him "Dimam ibn Sa`labah".

### S46
- **Title:** The Farewell Sermon
- **Text:** In 10 AH the Prophet ﷺ performed his Hajj with a vast gathering and spoke at ʿArafāt, warning he might not meet them the next year. He declared their lives, honour and wealth sacred to one another, cancelled the blood claims and interest of the days of ignorance, and said that holding to the Book of Allah and his Sunnah would keep them from going astray. Treat every Muslim's life, honour and wealth as sacred.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), p. 561 (pdf p. 591)

### S47
- **Title:** Glorifying and Seeking Forgiveness
- **Text:** After the verse "Today I have perfected your religion for you" and the revelation of Sūrat al-Nasr, the Prophet ﷺ prepared for the Hereafter. Wherever he was, sitting or walking, he repeated words like: glory be to Allah and praise be to Him; I seek Allah's forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance. That Ramadān he spent twenty days in iʿtikāf instead of ten. Keep your tongue busy with tasbīh and istighfār.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 562, 565–566 (pdf pp. 592, 595–596)

### S48
- **Title:** Choosing What Is with Allah
- **Text:** In his last days the Prophet ﷺ came to the masjid leaning on two men and spoke from the minbar: Allah had given a servant the choice between this world and what is with Him, and that servant had chosen what is with Allah. Abū Bakr understood that he meant himself, and wept. He urged kindness to the Ansār and said they would meet him at the Hawd. Choose what is with Allah over this world.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 570–572 (pdf pp. 600–602)

### S49
- **Title:** A Last Look at the Rows
- **Text:** When illness kept him from the masjid, the Prophet ﷺ had Abū Bakr lead the salāh. On the morning of his last day, a Monday, he raised the curtain of his room, saw his Companions standing in rows for Fajr and smiled. It was the last time they saw his blessed face. His last look at his Ummah found them praying in rows; let that be how we are found.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 572–574 (pdf pp. 602–604)

### S50
- **Title:** "The Highest Companion"
- **Text:** As death came, the Prophet ﷺ rested his head on ʿĀʾishah and used a miswāk she softened for him. He kept dipping his hand in water and wiping his face, saying: there is none worthy of worship but Allah; death has its pangs. Then he raised his hands and said: O Allah, the highest companion, and passed away on a Monday in Rabīʿ al-Awwal, aged sixty-three. Live so that lā ilāha illallāh is on your tongue at the end.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 574–575 (pdf pp. 604–605)

### S51
- **Title:** "Allah Is Ever-Living"
- **Text:** The news of the Prophet's passing left the Companions stunned with grief. Abū Bakr came, kissed his forehead, then told the people: whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died; whoever worships Allah, Allah is alive and does not die. He recited, "Muhammad is but a Messenger; messengers have passed away before him," and it was as if they heard it for the first time. In every loss, hold on to Allah.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 576–578 (pdf pp. 606–608)

### S52
- **Title:** Salawāt Without an Imam
- **Text:** The Prophet ﷺ was buried where he passed away. Group after group, men, then women, then children, came in to pray over him and send salawāt, each on their own with no imam; about thirty thousand took part. Abū Bakr and ʿUmar testified that he had conveyed all that was revealed and was kind and merciful to the believers. Allah and His angels send salawāt upon him; keep adding yours.
- **Source:** Seeratul Mustafa (Abridged), pp. 582–584 (pdf pp. 612–614)
