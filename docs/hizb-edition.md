# al-Hizb al-A'zam (الحزب الأعظم): which edition to transcribe from

Research date: 2026-11-07. Scope: Mulla 'Ali al-Qari's (d. 1014 AH) *al-Hizb al-A'zam wa al-Wird al-Afkham*, in its 7-manzil weekly arrangement.

## Recommendation

**Primary source: the Nomani takhrij edition**, published by Majlis al-Da'wa wa al-Tahqiq al-Islami, Allama Banuri Town, Karachi.
- Arabic text with takhrij by Shaykh Muhammad 'Abd al-Rashid al-Nu'mani (d. 1999).
- Urdu translation by Shaykh Muhammad Badr 'Alam Meerathi al-Madani (d. 1965).
- Scan: https://archive.org/details/al-hizb-al-azam-takhreej-nomani (direct PDF, about 4.6 MB: https://archive.org/download/al-hizb-al-azam-takhreej-nomani/Al-Hizb-al-Azam-Takhreej-Maulana-Abdur-Rasheed-Nomani.pdf)

Why this one:
1. It is the closest thing to a scholarly edition (muhaqqaq) in the Deobandi/South Asian tradition. Nu'mani was a leading Hanafi muhaddith, and the book comes from Banuri Town. He traced each du'a to its source, citing al-Durr al-Manthur, Kanz al-'Ummal and others.
   - In his introduction he says the takhrij was done at the request of Badr 'Alam Meerathi's circle in Madinah.
   - He also says only "four or five" hadiths could not be traced.
   - A Hanafi South Asian scholar will recognise and trust this lineage.
2. It is arranged in 7 manzils, and **Manzil 1 is يوم السبت (Saturday)**. The running heads read "منزل اول / یوم السبت". It ends on Friday, with the salawat.
3. Each du'a is numbered and boxed, with the Urdu meaning under each line. That makes it easy to segment into app entries and check off one by one.
4. The text has full harakat in Indo-Pak naskh, with Indo-Pak Qur'anic conventions (dagger alif, Indo-Pak sukun), which is what users in the subcontinent are used to.

Weaknesses:
- It is a CamScanner phone scan of an old lithographic-style print. It is legible, but some vowel marks are crowded.
- It is not OCR-able for Arabic, so it has to be typed by hand.
- The takhrij footnotes are unvowelled.
- The archive.org metadata gives the year as 1980. That is not verified from a colophon.

**Second copy to collate against: the Hazim Muhammad Dawud edition (Cairo, 2011).**
- Edited (i'tina bi-nususihi wa sharh gharibihi) by Hazim Muhammad Dawud, who is described as one of the 'ulama' of al-Azhar.
- Scan from the Bibliotheca Alexandrina: https://archive.org/details/AAlexandrina-297077 (PDF about 14.7 MB)
- It is clean modern typesetting, and the Qur'anic du'as are set in **Uthmani script with surah:ayah references**. Every du'a is a separate paragraph.
- It is the easiest copy to transcribe accurately. Use it to check the vowelling of the Qur'anic portions, and the Nomani edition for the order and content.

The **abridgements** (*Mukhtasar al-Hizb al-A'zam*) are not the full text and should not be used. They include the one by Sufi Muhammad Iqbal Madani, said to be prepared at Shaykh al-Hadith Zakariyya's suggestion, and the Idara Impex English version. Links:
- https://archive.org/details/mukhtasar-al-hizb-al-azam-sufi-iqbal
- https://archive.org/details/MukhtasarAlHizbAlAazam

I did not find a muhaqqaq edition made from manuscripts, with variant readings from multiple copies, or one with a printed endorsement by Maulana Zakariyya, Saharanpur or Deoband.

The Ottoman commentaries could be used to settle hard readings:
- al-Saqizi, *Fayd al-Arham*
- al-Izmiri, *Fath al-Rabb al-Akram*
- al-Madani al-Tarabzuni, *al-Durr al-Munazzam*

## Do the weekly divisions differ?

- **Who made the split:** al-Qari did not divide the book into days himself. His own introduction (printed in every edition) says: "فإن قدرت على قراءتها كل جمعة، وإلا ففي كل شهر، وإلا ففي كل سنة…". That means read the whole thing once a week (or month, or year). Hazim Dawud says plainly that "some scholars after him" divided it into seven parts for the days of the week, giving the salawat to Friday, and that "this division is not the author's work".
- **Start and end days:** every 7-day edition I could inspect (#1, #2, #3, #4) starts on **Saturday** and ends on **Friday** with the salawat. I found no edition that starts on another day.
- **Small differences inside a day:** within a day the order follows the mushaf in both #1 and #2, but how much of a verse each edition quotes can differ. For example, on Saturday, Nomani quotes Al 'Imran 191 alone, while Dawud gives Al 'Imran 191–194 as one passage. Exactly where each day ends may also differ slightly. When transcribing, mark every du'a with its source (surah:ayah, or hadith collection) and record any place where #1 and #2 disagree.

## Practical transcription notes

- **Text:** type the Arabic text yourself from #1, and check the Qur'anic vowelling against #2. Text you typed yourself carries none of the publishers' typesetting rights.
- **What not to copy:** don't reproduce Nu'mani's takhrij footnotes, Dawud's gharib notes, or any modern translation word for word. Short source tags like "Al 'Imran 191" or "Muslim" are facts and are fine to use.
- **Scans:** treat the archive.org uploads as reference copies. They are user uploads, not releases licensed by the publishers, so don't rehost them.
