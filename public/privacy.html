Privacy Policy

Effective 15 November 2026

Zad is a free tracker for the five daily prayers, good deeds (amal) and qada. Your worship is between you and Allah, so Zad is built to keep it private: by default everything stays on your phone. Data only leaves your phone when you turn on cloud backup, look up a city, or when the app sends a crash report, all explained below.

In short

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. We never sell your data or share it for advertising.
  • Your prayer logs stay on your phone unless you choose cloud backup. Cloud backup is optional and stored in your own private space on Google's Firebase service, in the United States.
  • Your logs show your religious practice, and excused days (hayd or nifās) can reveal health information. We only back these up after you clearly agree, and you can delete them at any time from inside the app.
  • Location is rounded to about 1 km before it is saved or sent anywhere.
  • Crash reports contain technical details only, never your logs, and you can turn them off.
  • You can see, export, correct and delete your data. Questions: zadapp.team@gmail.com.

Contact: zadapp.team@gmail.com. We are a one-person team, so we have not appointed a data protection officer; please send any privacy question to that email and we will answer it ourselves.

This policy covers the Zad app for Android and iPhone (app ID com.zad.app on both), offered on Google Play and the App Store, and the Zad pages at arahkhan99.github.io/amal-tracker.

What stays on your phone

Zad saves its data in the app's own storage on your phone. We cannot see it. This includes:

  • The name you type for the greeting on Home (optional).
  • Your prayer logs (for example on time, in jamāʿah, made up, missed), sunnah and Witr logs, and your good-deeds logs, including counts and notes you add (such as a Quran position or a sadaqah note) and your own list of good deeds.
  • Excused days (hayd or nifās), if you use that feature, and whether Zad is set up for a brother or a sister, if you choose one.
  • Qada balances for prayers and fasts, your monthly goals and their history.
  • Your settings: madhhab, reminder choices, prayer-time calculation and Asr method, Hijri date choices, app lock on or off, crash reports on or off, and your approximate location (rounded to about 1 km) with a city label.
  • If you use cloud backup: the version of the Terms and Privacy Policy you agreed to, your agreement to back up your records, and when you agreed.

It is removed when you use Settings → Reset all data or uninstall the app.

Things that show on your screen. Reminders and Zad's home-screen widgets show prayer names, times and whether today's prayers are logged. Anyone who can see your screen may see them. You can turn reminders off in Settings and remove widgets from your home screen.

Your phone's own backups. Android's backup to your Google account and iPhone's iCloud backup may include Zad's data, depending on your phone settings. Those backups are run by Google or Apple under your own account; we cannot see or delete them.

Files you export. Files you create with Settings → Back up or Export my data (CSV) are saved or shared wherever you choose. Where they go after that is up to you.

Optional cloud backup

If you turn on Settings → Cloud backup (or choose "Continue with Google" or "Continue with Apple" when you first open Zad), you sign in with your Google account or Apple ID and your tracker data is backed up and synced between your phones. This uses two services from Google's Firebase:

  • Firebase Authentication handles the Google or Apple sign-in. It keeps your account's email address (with Apple this can be a private relay address if you chose "Hide My Email"), a user ID, the name (and, for Google, the profile-photo link) shared at sign-in, and when you created the account and last signed in. Google also logs the IP address used to sign in for a few weeks, for security.
  • Cloud Firestore stores a copy of everything listed under "What stays on your phone" above, under your user ID, with the time of each save. It is hosted on Google Cloud in the United States (multi-region nam5). Firebase Authentication also runs in the United States.

Your agreement. Before you sign in, Zad explains that your prayer records show your religious practice, may include excused days, and will be stored on Google's servers in the United States, and asks you to tick a separate box to agree to back them up. The sign-in buttons stay off until you tick it, and nothing is uploaded before you agree. Signing in also means you accept the Terms of use. Zad saves which version you agreed to and when. If these documents change in a way that matters, the app asks you again and pauses syncing until you answer; choosing Sign out, keep data on this phone stops cloud backup and keeps everything on your phone.

Who can see it. Security rules on the database let each signed-in account read and write only its own data. No other user can see it, and the app has no feature to look at other people's data. As the developer we manage the Firebase project, so we could technically open the database. We do not look at your data, except when you ask us to (for example to delete your account or fix a sync problem you report) or when the law requires it. Google stores the data for us as our service provider. Data travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS) and Google encrypts it at rest.

Sign out stops syncing on that phone and keeps the data on the phone. Your cloud copy stays until you delete it.

Settings → Delete my account (shown while you are signed in) permanently deletes your cloud backup and your Zad sign-in from inside the app. You can also ask us by email. See Delete your account and data. Withdrawing your agreement is the same thing: delete your account (or ask us to), and we stop holding your records.

Sensitive information

Some of what Zad holds is especially private, and the law treats it with extra care:

  • Religious practice. Prayer, good-deeds and qada logs, and your madhhab, show your religious beliefs and practice.
  • Health information. If you mark excused days for hayd or nifās, those days can reveal information about menstruation or recent childbirth. In some places (for example Washington State and Nevada in the US) this counts as "consumer health data".

We only use this information to run the features you see: showing your logs and progress, working out qada, scheduling reminders, and backing up and syncing if you choose. We never sell it, never use it for advertising or profiling, never share it with anyone except Google as our storage provider for cloud backup, and never put it in crash reports. It only leaves your phone if you agree to cloud backup. If you'd rather not store excused days at all, turn off Settings → Excused days.

Location

Prayer times are calculated on your phone from your location. You can allow location access or type a city instead.

  • Approximate location only: when you choose to use your location, Zad reads your position once and straight away rounds it to two decimal places, which is within about 1 km of where you are. Only this rounded location is saved, backed up or sent anywhere. The precise reading is not stored or sent. A city you pick from search is rounded the same way. If "Update location when I travel" is on (Settings, under Location; it applies only when you chose "Use my current location"), Zad checks your approximate location when it opens, only if you have already allowed location access, rounds it the same way, and looks up the city name the same way if you have moved more than 25 km. Your location is never tracked in the background.
  • City name: when you are online, the rounded coordinates are sent once to BigDataCloud's reverse-geocoding service to look up your city's name. If you are offline, Zad shows "Your location" instead.
  • City search: if you search for a city, the text you type is sent to the Open-Meteo geocoding service to find matching places.

These services receive the request and, like any website, your IP address. We do not send them your name, account or logs, and we do not keep anything from these lookups apart from the result you choose.

Reminders and app lock

Prayer reminders are scheduled on the phone itself. They are not sent through any server. The optional app lock uses your phone's own fingerprint, face or screen-lock prompt. Zad only learns "unlocked" or "not unlocked" and never sees your fingerprint or face data.

Crash reports

To find and fix bugs, the app sends a report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google) when it crashes or hits an error. A report contains the device model, operating system version, app version, the technical error message and stack trace (which part of the app's code failed), the screen that was open (for example "home" or "settings"), the light or dark theme setting, whether the phone is Android or iPhone, and random installation IDs used to count crashes. Like any internet request, Google also sees your IP address. A report does not contain your name, email, account, location, prayer or deeds logs, notes, excused days, qada or madhhab, and it is not linked to your cloud backup. Crashlytics keeps reports for 90 days and then deletes them.

Crash reports are off until you agree. Zad asks you when you first open it whether to send crash reports, and you can change your answer at any time with Settings → Send crash reports. When you turn them off, reports not yet sent are deleted, and the change takes full effect the next time Zad opens.

Moon-sighting committees

If you follow a moon-sighting committee for the Hijri date while signed in, Zad downloads that committee's published month start dates. People invited by us to update a committee's dates have their user ID saved next to each date they enter, so changes can be traced. Nothing about other users is saved.

Feedback and "report a mistake"

If you send us feedback or report a mistake from inside the app (Help & tour → Send feedback, or Report a mistake on a hadith, adhkar or Quran page), we receive your message, the kind of feedback, the item and parts you were reporting (if any) and the time. The app version, whether your phone is Android or iPhone, and the phone model are added only while the "Include app and device details" box is ticked (it starts ticked, and you can untick it before sending). Nothing else from the tracker is ever attached: no prayer logs, notes, excused days or location.

If you are signed in, the message is saved in our Firebase database (Cloud Firestore, United States) together with your account's user ID, which is always attached so we know which messages are yours. Adding an email address is optional; we only use it to reply to you. Your messages are deleted together with your account when you use Settings → Delete my account. If you are not signed in, Zad opens your own email app with the message ready to send, so it reaches us as a normal email from your address, and we see your email address.

Please don't include anything more personal than you need to, such as details of your own worship or health. We keep feedback for up to 12 months after we have dealt with it, then delete it. Ask us at any time to delete a message sooner.

What we don't do

  • No advertising and no advertising IDs.
  • No analytics or tracking tools. The only diagnostics are the crash reports described above.
  • We do not sell your personal data, and we do not "share" it for cross-context behavioural advertising (as California law uses that word).
  • We do not use your data to make automated decisions about you or to build a profile of you.
  • We do not access your contacts, photos, microphone or camera.

Why we use your data (legal bases)

For people in the EU, the UK and other places with similar laws:

  • Cloud backup of your records, including religious-practice and health information: your explicit consent, given when you agree before signing in. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account.
  • Your Google sign-in (email, user ID, sign-in times): needed to provide the backup you asked for (contract).
  • City lookups and search: needed to provide the feature you just asked for (contract).
  • Crash reports: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working, using technical data only, with a switch to turn them off.
  • Feedback you send: our legitimate interest in answering you and fixing mistakes.
  • Records of your agreement and of deletion requests: to meet our legal obligations.

Where your data is stored

Cloud backup and sign-in data is stored in the United States. Crash reports may be processed wherever Google runs its services. If you live outside the US, your data is transferred there. Google protects these transfers with the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (and the UK equivalent) in its Firebase data processing terms, and Google LLC takes part in the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. You can ask us for more details.

How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you reset the app or uninstall it.
  • Cloud backup and sign-in: until you delete your account. Settings → Delete my account deletes it straight away. If you ask by email, we delete it within 30 days. Google then clears remaining copies from its backup systems within about 180 days; nobody can use them in the meantime.
  • Sign-in IP addresses: a few weeks (kept by Google for security).
  • Crash reports: 90 days.
  • Emails you send us: as long as needed to help you, and no longer than 2 years, except a short record of deletion or privacy requests, which we keep to show we handled them.

If a cloud account looks abandoned, we may delete it; we will email you first if we do.

Your choices and rights

  • Use Zad without signing in. Everything then stays on your phone.
  • Turn location off and type a city instead.
  • Turn crash reports off with Settings → Send crash reports.
  • Turn off excused days with Settings → Excused days.
  • Get a copy of your data at any time with Settings → Back up (a complete file you can restore or keep) or Export my data (a spreadsheet).
  • Correct anything by editing it in the app; changes sync to your backup.
  • Delete your cloud backup and sign-in at any time with Settings → Delete my account.
  • Ask us for a copy of what we hold about you (including your sign-in details), a correction, deletion, or to restrict or object to how we use it, by emailing zadapp.team@gmail.com.

We reply within one month (within 45 days where US state law allows that). We may ask you to confirm the request comes from you, usually by writing from the Google account email you signed in with. You can ask someone to make a request for you; we may ask for proof they are allowed to. We will never treat you differently for using your rights, and you can appeal any decision by replying to our answer.

If you are in the EU, the UK or Switzerland

You have the rights of access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, data portability and to withdraw consent at any time (this doesn't affect what was done before). You can also complain to your local data protection authority, for example the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK, although we'd be grateful for the chance to fix things first.

If you are in the United States

Depending on your state, you have rights to know, access, correct, delete and get a copy of your personal data, and to opt out of sale, targeted advertising and profiling. We don't do any of those things, so there is nothing to opt out of. We only use sensitive data (religious beliefs and health information) to provide the features you ask for, and with your consent where the law requires it. We don't use Do Not Track signals or Global Privacy Control to change anything, because we don't track you across apps or websites in the first place.

Consumer health data (Washington, Nevada, Connecticut and similar laws). The only health-related data Zad may hold is excused days you mark (hayd or nifās), and the fact that a prayer is logged as excused. We collect it only when you choose to use that feature, keep it on your phone unless you consent to cloud backup, use it only to show your tracker correctly, and never sell it or share it with anyone except Google as our storage provider. You can see, export and delete it as described above, and withdraw consent by deleting your account.

If you are in Canada, India, Pakistan, the Gulf, Malaysia or elsewhere

You have the rights your local law gives you, which usually include access, correction and withdrawing consent. Email us and we will help, whatever country you live in. In India, the email address above is also our contact for grievances.

Children and teenagers

Zad is meant for adults and teenagers. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. Cloud backup is only for people who are at least 13, or older where local law requires (for example 16 in some European countries, 18 in India), unless a parent or guardian has agreed. Teenagers should check with a parent before turning on cloud backup. If you believe a child has used cloud backup without permission, email us and we will delete the data. Zad has no public profiles, chat or sharing, and location is approximate and never shown to anyone.

Security

We use encrypted connections, Google's encryption at rest, database rules that lock each account to its owner, and an optional app lock on your phone. No system is perfectly secure, so please keep your Google account and phone protected. If a security incident affects your data, we will tell you and the authorities where the law requires.

Changes

For important changes, especially about cloud backup or sensitive data, the app will ask you again before continuing to back up, and the release notes will mention it. We will never start using your existing data in a new way that needs your consent without asking first.
